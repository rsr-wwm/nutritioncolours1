# Dyslipidemia Nutrition Guide – Thingdol, Mizoram

> **Clinical target biomarker:** TSH, Free T3, Free T4 & Anti-TPO Antibodies

## Why Thingdol Residents Are at Higher Risk for Dyslipidemia

Residents of **Thingdol, Mizoram** face a unique metabolic challenge shaped by the local tropical monsoonal climate, wet summers, mild winters, dietary staples such as *Polished rice, seafood, refined palm oil, low dietary fiber*, and a circadian challenge of monsoon season humidity shifts affecting digestive transit speed and enzyme secretion.

Constant tropical heat increases fluid and electrolyte turnover, requiring trace mineral replenishment to sustain cellular pumps.

## Key Nutritional Deficiency to Address

**Vitamin D3 (due to low sunlight synthesis or indoor shift hours)** is a prevalent deficiency among Thingdol residents. Addressing this gap is a critical first step in managing Dyslipidemia effectively.

## Recommended Dietary Shifts

**Replace** your current staples:
- ❌ Polished rice, seafood, refined palm oil, low dietary fiber

**With** evidence-based alternatives:
- ✅ Black Kavuni rice, wild-caught seafood, cold-pressed sesame oil, and fiber-rich local leafy greens.

## Circadian Nutrition for Thingdol

**Your local circadian challenge:** Monsoon season humidity shifts affecting digestive transit speed and enzyme secretion

Aligning meal timing with your body's natural clock is especially important in Thingdol's tropical monsoonal climate climate:

- 🌅 Eat your **largest meal between 12:00 PM – 2:00 PM** (peak metabolic window)
- 🌙 Avoid heavy meals after **7:00 PM**
- 🥚 Front-load protein and fibre at breakfast to prevent the mid-day insulin spike
