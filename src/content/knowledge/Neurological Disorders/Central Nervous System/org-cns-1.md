## Overview

The brain is organized into major divisions — the cerebrum, cerebellum, brainstem, and diencephalon — with the cerebral cortex further divided into frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital lobes, each responsible for distinct functions such as executive control, sensory processing, memory, and vision. Deeper structures include the basal ganglia (involved in movement control and implicated in Parkinson's and Huntington's disease), the limbic system (involved in memory and emotion), and the blood-brain barrier, which selectively regulates what substances can pass from the bloodstream into brain tissue. Neuroplasticity — the brain's capacity to reorganize and form new connections — underlies learning and recovery after injury.
