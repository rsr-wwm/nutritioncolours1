## Overview

This category spans several distinct brain disorders: stroke (ischemic and hemorrhagic), Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, traumatic brain injury (TBI), and multiple sclerosis. Each has a different underlying cause, prognosis, and treatment approach — vascular for stroke, neurodegenerative for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, electrical for epilepsy, traumatic and structural for TBI, and autoimmune for MS — so they are best understood, and managed, individually rather than as a single group.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Because these conditions differ so much, nutrition's relevance varies by disease rather than applying uniformly. Real, evidence-based associations exist for several: Mediterranean and DASH-style diets for stroke risk reduction; the MIND diet's association with slower cognitive decline relevant to Alzheimer's; the ketogenic diet as a medically supervised adjunct for drug-resistant epilepsy; and protein-timing strategies relevant to levodopa absorption in Parkinson's disease. Traumatic brain injury requires acute nutritional support tailored to injury severity, managed by the hospital care team — general dietary advice is not applicable in the acute phase. See the dedicated pages for stroke, Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and multiple sclerosis for the specific, disease-relevant nutritional evidence.

## Safety Note

Nutrition is supportive, not curative, for any of these conditions, and dietary changes should always be discussed with the treating neurologist, particularly for epilepsy and acute TBI, where self-directed dietary changes carry real risk.
