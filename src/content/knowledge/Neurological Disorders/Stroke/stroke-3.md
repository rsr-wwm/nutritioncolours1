## Overview

Preventing a first or repeat stroke relies on managing the underlying risk factors: antiplatelet medications (aspirin, clopidogrel, sometimes short-term dual antiplatelet therapy) reduce clot formation, statins (such as atorvastatin) lower cholesterol and stabilize arterial plaque, and for significant carotid artery narrowing, carotid endarterectomy or stenting can reduce stroke risk surgically. For patients with atrial fibrillation, anticoagulation is essential to prevent clots forming in the heart from traveling to the brain. Lifestyle modification is consistently listed alongside these medical therapies as a core pillar of stroke prevention.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Diet is one of the best-evidenced, most directly relevant lifestyle factors in stroke prevention. The Mediterranean diet and the DASH eating pattern — rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, fish, nuts, and olive oil, and low in sodium, red meat, and refined sugar — have strong evidence for reducing blood pressure, improving cholesterol, and lowering stroke risk, and are recommended by cardiovascular and stroke prevention guidelines as complementary to, not a replacement for, prescribed antiplatelet or statin therapy. Sodium reduction specifically targets blood pressure control, the single largest modifiable stroke risk factor. For patients on statins, there's no need for a specialized "statin diet," though a heart-healthy dietary pattern supports the cholesterol-lowering goals these medications aim for. Patients on warfarin should maintain consistent (not necessarily low) vitamin K intake, since fluctuating intake of vitamin K-rich foods like leafy greens can affect anticoagulation control — this is a genuine, well-established interaction.

## Safety Note

Diet supports but does not replace antiplatelet, statin, or anticoagulant therapy, or surgical interventions like carotid endarterectomy when medically indicated. Patients on warfarin should discuss vitamin K intake consistency with their care team rather than eliminating vitamin K-rich foods. Any new stroke symptoms require immediate emergency evaluation regardless of preventive measures already in place.
