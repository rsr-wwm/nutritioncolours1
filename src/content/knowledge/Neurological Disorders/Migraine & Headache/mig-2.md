## Overview

For people with frequent or disabling migraine, preventive treatment aims to reduce attack frequency and severity. CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab/Aimovig, fremanezumab/Ajovy, galcanezumab/Emgality, eptinezumab) and oral CGRP antagonists (atogepant, rimegepant used preventively) are newer options that target the calcitonin gene-related peptide pathway involved in migraine. Older preventive options include beta-blockers, topiramate, and amitriptyline. OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox) is approved specifically for chronic migraine (15 or more headache days per month), and neuromodulation devices offer a non-drug option for some patients.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Alongside pharmacologic prevention, magnesium and riboflavin (vitamin B2) have real, guideline-recognized evidence as possibly effective preventive supplements for migraine, and are reasonable to discuss with a headache specialist as an adjunct — not a replacement — for prevention, especially in patients wanting to minimize medication burden. Consistent sleep and meal schedules support prevention broadly, since irregular routines and skipped meals are common triggers. Identifying and moderating individual dietary triggers (commonly alcohol, caffeine fluctuations, and certain aged or processed foods) through a headache diary is more useful than following a one-size-fits-all elimination diet, since triggers are highly individual.

## Safety Note

Nutritional and supplement strategies support but do not replace prescribed preventive therapy, particularly for chronic or disabling migraine. Any supplement use, including magnesium or riboflavin, should be discussed with the prescribing physician to check for interactions with CGRP inhibitors or other migraine medications. New or changing headache patterns should always be evaluated medically.
