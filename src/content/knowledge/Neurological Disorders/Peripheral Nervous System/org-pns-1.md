## Overview

The peripheral nervous system includes the 12 pairs of cranial nerves, the 31 pairs of spinal nerves, the nerve plexuses (cervical, brachial, lumbar, sacral) formed where spinal nerves converge and redistribute, and the autonomic nervous system (sympathetic "fight or flight," parasympathetic "rest and digest," and enteric systems governing involuntary functions). Nerve signals are transmitted via myelinated axons, and damage to these peripheral nerves — from causes including diabetes, chemotherapy, B12 deficiency, or autoimmune conditions like Guillain-Barré syndrome — results in peripheral neuropathy.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Vitamin B12 deficiency is a genuine, well-documented cause of peripheral nerve damage, since B12 is essential for maintaining the myelin sheath that insulates nerve fibers. This is more than an anatomy footnote — it is a real, correctable cause of neuropathy symptoms in people who are deficient, particularly older adults, those on long-term metformin, and people following vegan diets without supplementation.
