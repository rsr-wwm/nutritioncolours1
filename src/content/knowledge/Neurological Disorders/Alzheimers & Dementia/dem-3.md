## Overview

Not all dementia is Alzheimer's disease. Vascular dementia results from reduced blood flow to the brain, often following stroke or chronic small-vessel disease, and shares risk factors with cardiovascular disease. Lewy body dementia involves abnormal protein deposits (Lewy bodies) and overlaps clinically with Parkinson's disease, causing fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, and movement symptoms. Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) typically presents earlier in life with changes in personality, behavior, or language rather than memory loss first. Normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH) is a potentially treatable cause of gait disturbance, cognitive decline, and urinary incontinence due to excess cerebrospinal fluid. A subset of cognitive impairment is reversible, most notably when caused by vitamin B12 deficiency, thyroid dysfunction, or depression — identifying these is a critical part of any dementia workup.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Because vascular dementia shares its underlying pathology with stroke and cardiovascular disease, dietary strategies proven to reduce cardiovascular risk are directly relevant here: reducing sodium, limiting saturated fat, and following a Mediterranean- or DASH-style eating pattern to support healthy blood pressure and cholesterol. Vitamin B12 deficiency is a well-documented, reversible cause of cognitive impairment and peripheral nerve symptoms; it is more common in older adults, those on long-term metformin or acid-reducing medications, and people following strict vegan diets without supplementation, so screening and correction (dietary or supplemental, as directed by a clinician) can meaningfully improve symptoms. There is no specific proven diet for Lewy body dementia or FTD, though good general nutrition supports overall function. As any dementia advances, dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) becomes more common and increases aspiration risk — texture-modified diets and swallowing evaluations by a speech-language pathologist become important safety measures.

## Safety Note

A B12 or thyroid-related cause of cognitive symptoms should always be ruled out by a physician before assuming a diagnosis of irreversible dementia, since these causes can genuinely improve with treatment. Diet does not treat vascular, Lewy body, or frontotemporal dementia directly, and any swallowing difficulty warrants prompt clinical evaluation rather than self-managed dietary changes.
