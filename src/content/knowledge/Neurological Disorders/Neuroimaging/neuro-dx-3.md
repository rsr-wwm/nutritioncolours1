## Overview

Electroencephalography (EEG) records the brain's electrical activity through electrodes placed on the scalp, distinguishing normal rhythms (alpha, beta, theta, delta) from abnormal epileptiform discharges (spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave patterns) that support an epilepsy diagnosis. EEG also identifies distinct sleep stages (N1, N2, N3, REM) and can be extended over hours or days (ambulatory EEG) to capture infrequent events, sometimes using activation procedures like hyperventilation or photic stimulation to provoke abnormal patterns.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

This is a diagnostic-testing topic and outside the scope of a nutrition review — EEG results guide diagnosis and treatment decisions and have no nutritional component themselves. For patients diagnosed with epilepsy via EEG, the real, evidence-based nutrition connection is the ketogenic diet as a medically supervised adjunct treatment for drug-resistant epilepsy, discussed in detail on the epilepsy and seizure disorder pages.
