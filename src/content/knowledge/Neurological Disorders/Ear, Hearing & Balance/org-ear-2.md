## Overview

Hearing and balance disorders covered here include hearing loss (conductive or sensorineural), tinnitus (ringing or buzzing with no external sound source), Meniere's disease (a disorder of the inner ear causing episodic vertigo, fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus, and aural fullness, thought to involve excess fluid pressure — endolymphatic hydrops), vertigo from causes such as BPPV or vestibular neuritis, and ear infections. Management ranges from hearing aids and the Epley maneuver (for BPPV) to medication and, for Meniere's disease, dietary and pharmacologic approaches to reduce fluid pressure.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Meniere's disease has a genuine, well-established dietary connection: a low-sodium diet is a standard first-line recommendation, based on the idea that reducing sodium helps limit fluid retention in the inner ear and may reduce the frequency or severity of vertigo episodes; many clinicians also suggest limiting caffeine and alcohol, though the evidence for those is less robust than for sodium restriction. For tinnitus, no diet has been proven to cure or consistently reduce symptoms, though some patients report caffeine or alcohol as personal triggers worth tracking. For hearing loss, ear infections, and vertigo from causes like BPPV, diet has no established direct role — treatment is medical, procedural (Epley maneuver), or surgical as appropriate.

## Safety Note

Dietary sodium restriction for Meniere's disease should be part of a plan discussed with an ENT specialist, alongside other management strategies. New or severe vertigo, sudden hearing loss, or symptoms suggestive of a central cause (such as stroke) require prompt medical evaluation.
