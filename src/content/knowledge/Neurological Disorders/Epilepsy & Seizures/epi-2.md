## Overview

Status epilepticus is a life-threatening neurological emergency defined as a seizure lasting longer than five minutes, or repeated seizures without full recovery of consciousness in between. It can be convulsive or non-convulsive, and requires immediate emergency treatment to prevent permanent brain injury or death. First-line treatment is intravenous benzodiazepines (lorazepam, midazolam), followed by IV antiseizure medications (fosphenytoin, levetiracetam, valproate) if seizures continue. Refractory status epilepticus that does not respond to these measures requires anesthetic agents (propofol, midazolam infusion) in an intensive care setting, and super-refractory cases may require prolonged, specialized ICU management.

## Nutritional and Dietary Considerations

Status epilepticus is a medical emergency managed in a hospital or ICU setting — there is no role for dietary self-management during an acute episode. In highly specialized centers, a ketogenic diet is sometimes used as an adjunct treatment for super-refractory status epilepticus that has not responded to standard medications, but this is an intensive, medically supervised intervention delivered under continuous monitoring, not something to attempt outside of that setting. Outside of acute crises, for patients with a known epilepsy diagnosis, general nutritional stability — consistent meals, adequate sleep, and avoiding known personal seizure triggers — supports overall seizure control, though this does not prevent status epilepticus in every case.

## Safety Note

Status epilepticus is a medical emergency: any seizure lasting more than five minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery, requires immediate emergency medical services (call for emergency help right away). Diet and nutrition play no role in acute management and must never delay emergency treatment. Any dietary intervention for seizure disorders should only be pursued under direct neurology supervision.
