## Overview

The endocrine system is a network of glands that release hormones directly into the bloodstream to regulate metabolism, growth, reproduction, and the body's response to stress. The major glands include:

- **Pituitary gland** — sits at the base of the brain and directs several other glands by releasing hormones like ACTH, TSH, and growth hormone.
- **Thyroid gland** — in the neck, produces T3 and T4, which set the body's metabolic rate.
- **Parathyroid glands** — four small glands behind the thyroid that regulate blood calcium levels.
- **Adrenal glands** — sit atop the kidneys and produce cortisol (the "stress hormone") and aldosterone (which manages sodium and fluid balance).
- **Pancreas** — produces insulin and glucagon, which control blood sugar.
- **Gonads** (ovaries and testes) — produce estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone, which drive reproductive function.

## Why this matters for nutrition

Several of these glands have well-established nutrient links: the thyroid requires adequate (not excessive) dietary iodine to make its hormones; the parathyroid-bone axis depends on adequate calcium and vitamin D; and the pancreas's blood sugar regulation is directly influenced by carbohydrate intake and eating patterns. Because the glands interact in feedback loops, a problem in one gland can affect nutrient needs elsewhere in the system.
