## Overview

Most hormones are controlled by negative feedback: as a hormone's level rises, it signals the gland (often via the brain's hypothalamus and pituitary) to slow production, keeping levels within a normal range. A few processes, such as the hormone surge that triggers ovulation, instead use positive feedback, where rising hormone levels briefly amplify further release. Hormones act by binding to specific receptors — some on the outside of target cells, others inside the cell — which then trigger the cell's response.

## Why this matters for nutrition

This feedback system is sensitive to the body's overall nutritional and energy status. For example, very low energy intake or very low body fat can suppress the reproductive hormone axis, which is one reason significant caloric restriction can disrupt menstrual cycles. This is a general physiological principle, not a basis for adjusting hormone medication or diagnosing a specific hormone imbalance from diet alone.

## Safety

This is general physiology information, not medical advice. Suspected hormone imbalances should be evaluated with blood tests ordered by a doctor, not inferred from symptoms or diet alone.
