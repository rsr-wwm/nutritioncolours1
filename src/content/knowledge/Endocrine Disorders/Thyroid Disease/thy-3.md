## Overview

Thyroid nodules are common and most are benign, typically evaluated through ultrasound and, if indicated, fine needle aspiration (FNA) using the Bethesda classification system. When thyroid cancer (papillary is the most common type) is diagnosed, treatment usually involves surgery (partial or total thyroidectomy), sometimes followed by radioactive iodine (RAI) ablation and long-term TSH suppression therapy. Nutrition's most specific role here is the temporary low-iodine diet used before RAI.

## Nutrition considerations

- **Iodine and nodule development**: Both chronically low and chronically excessive iodine intake have been linked in research to a higher prevalence of thyroid nodules, which is part of why NIH ODS emphasizes meeting — not exceeding — the adult RDA of 150 mcg/day rather than either extreme.
- **Low-iodine diet before RAI**: Patients preparing for radioactive iodine ablation are typically placed on a low-iodine diet for one to two weeks beforehand, under the direction of their oncology or nuclear medicine team, to maximize the treatment's effectiveness. This means avoiding iodized salt, seaweed, and certain processed foods and supplements — a specific, supervised, time-limited protocol.
- **After total thyroidectomy**: Since parathyroid glands sit adjacent to the thyroid and can be affected during surgery, calcium levels are monitored closely post-operatively; calcium supplementation, if needed, is guided by lab results rather than general dietary advice.
- **No evidence-based "anti-cancer diet"**: There is no established specific diet shown to treat thyroid cancer; general balanced nutrition supports recovery and overall health during and after treatment.

## Safety note

All treatment decisions — surgery, RAI, and TSH suppression targets — are made by an oncology/endocrinology team based on cancer type and stage. The low-iodine diet should only be followed as specifically instructed and for the duration prescribed by that team, not adopted independently.
