## Overview

Because hormone levels can shift throughout the day and in response to stress, meals, or medications, doctors sometimes need more than a single blood draw to diagnose an endocrine disorder. Dynamic (or "challenge") tests measure how a gland responds after it's stimulated or suppressed with a specific substance — for example, an ACTH stimulation test checks whether the adrenal glands can raise cortisol on demand, a dexamethasone suppression test checks whether cortisol production correctly shuts off, and an oral glucose tolerance test checks how the body handles a sugar load. These tests help confirm conditions such as adrenal insufficiency, Cushing's syndrome, or diabetes.

## What this means for patients

Many of these tests require fasting or specific timing (for example, no food for a set number of hours beforehand, or an early-morning appointment), since eating can affect the hormone being measured. Some may also require pausing certain medications first. The exact prep instructions depend on which test is ordered, so patients should follow what their endocrinology team specifies rather than a generic guideline.

## Safety

Which test (or combination of tests) is appropriate, and how the results should be interpreted, is a clinical decision made by an endocrinologist based on symptoms and initial labs. This page is general background information, not medical advice.
