## Overview

Diabetic retinopathy (eye), neuropathy (nerve), and nephropathy (kidney) are microvascular complications that develop from sustained high blood glucose over years. NIDDK and ADA materials are consistent on the central point: keeping blood glucose and blood pressure within target ranges over time is the primary way to reduce the risk and slow the progression of these complications. Nutrition's role here is largely about supporting that overall glycemic and cardiovascular control, rather than treating each complication with a separate diet.

## Nutrition considerations

- **Glycemic control as the foundation**: The same individualized, carbohydrate-aware eating pattern used for general type 2 diabetes management is the relevant nutrition strategy for reducing complication risk — there is no separate "retinopathy diet" or "neuropathy diet" with established evidence.
- **Blood pressure and sodium**: Because hypertension accelerates diabetic kidney and eye disease, a DASH-style, moderate-sodium eating pattern is commonly recommended alongside glucose management.
- **Kidney-specific nutrition, only if diagnosed**: If nephropathy is present, a nephrologist or renal dietitian may recommend individualized protein, potassium, or phosphorus adjustments — these are clinical decisions based on lab values and kidney function stage, not a generic template that applies to everyone with diabetes.
- **Peripheral neuropathy and foot care**: While no specific nutrient has strong evidence for reversing neuropathy, maintaining stable glucose and adequate B-vitamin status (particularly relevant if on long-term metformin, which can affect B12 absorption) is a reasonable, evidence-aligned focus — confirmed with lab testing rather than assumed.

## Safety note

These are serious, potentially vision- and organ-threatening complications. Any dietary changes — especially around protein, potassium, or phosphorus — should be individualized by the treating specialist based on actual lab results and disease stage, not applied generically.
