## Overview

Living well with CKD, especially in earlier stages, generally means adapting everyday habits rather than overhauling life entirely: staying physically active within a doctor's guidance, avoiding tobacco and limiting alcohol, keeping regular follow-up appointments, and attending to the real mental health toll a chronic diagnosis can carry — CKD is associated with higher rates of anxiety and depression, which can also affect how well someone manages their diet and medications.

## Nutritional considerations

Day-to-day eating with CKD works best as a sustainable, moderate pattern rather than a series of extreme swings — crash diets or unsupervised elimination diets can create nutrient gaps without any proven kidney benefit. General habits that align with NIDDK/NKF guidance include favoring fresh over heavily processed and packaged foods (which lowers sodium and phosphate-additive exposure), keeping regular meal timing to support stable blood pressure and blood sugar, and following individualized fluid guidance rather than a generic "drink more water" or "restrict water" rule, since fluid needs differ by CKD stage and swelling status. Moderate physical activity, as approved by a treating physician, supports blood pressure, blood sugar, and cardiovascular health, and can also support appetite and mood.

## Safety note

Persistent low mood, anxiety, or difficulty coping with a CKD diagnosis is common and worth raising directly with the care team — mental health support (counseling, and medication where appropriate) is part of standard CKD care, not a separate concern to manage alone.
