## Overview

CKD management shifts for populations with different physiological needs. Pregnancy in CKD is considered higher-risk and requires close joint monitoring by nephrology and obstetrics, since pregnancy itself increases kidney workload. CKD in children is often driven by congenital or genetic conditions and requires attention to growth alongside kidney protection, typically under a pediatric nephrologist. Older adults with CKD (kidney function naturally declines somewhat with age) face a different balance of risks, including a higher chance of medication side effects and under-nutrition from over-restriction.

## Nutritional considerations

- **Pregnancy**: Caloric and protein needs increase in pregnancy generally, which must be balanced carefully against a mother's kidney function and any pre-existing dietary limits — this balance is individualized and supervised jointly by nephrology and obstetric teams, not self-managed.
- **Children**: Pediatric CKD nutrition prioritizes supporting normal growth and development, so restriction is applied cautiously and only where labs clearly indicate a need, under a pediatric nephrology/dietitian team.
- **Older adults**: Because unintentional weight loss and malnutrition are common and harmful in older CKD patients, guidance for this group tends to avoid unnecessarily strict protein or other restrictions unless labs specifically warrant them, and pays close attention to medication interactions (many drugs need dose adjustment as kidney function declines with age).

## Safety note

None of these adjustments — pregnancy nutrition, pediatric growth-focused diets, or relaxed restriction in older adults — should be made without the relevant specialist team, since the right balance depends on individual kidney function, growth status, or frailty, which vary widely within each group.
