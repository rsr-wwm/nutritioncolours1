## Overview

The pace at which CKD progresses varies widely, but several well-established factors influence it: blood pressure control, blood glucose control in people with diabetes, avoiding nephrotoxic exposures, and, for some patients, moderate dietary protein adjustment. The NIDDK and KDOQI guidelines frame progression-slowing as a combination of medication (notably ACE inhibitors/ARBs and, where appropriate, SGLT2 inhibitors) and lifestyle measures working together, not either alone.

## Nutritional considerations

- **Blood pressure**: Sodium reduction is one of the most consistently supported dietary levers for slowing CKD-related kidney damage, since it directly supports blood pressure control.
- **Blood glucose**: In patients with diabetes-related kidney disease, consistent carbohydrate intake and glycemic management support slower progression alongside diabetes medications.
- **Protein**: Avoiding excessive high-protein diets or protein supplements is a reasonable general precaution; some evidence supports modest protein reduction slowing progression in select pre-dialysis patients, but this should only be attempted under a renal dietitian's supervision, since too little protein carries its own risks (muscle loss, malnutrition).
- **Other habits**: Maintaining a healthy weight, staying adequately hydrated per individual guidance, avoiding regular NSAID use, and not smoking are all supported general measures for protecting remaining kidney function.

## Safety note

There is no fixed protein gram target, sodium number, or supplement regimen proven to slow CKD progression the same way for everyone — the right combination depends on a person's stage, cause of CKD, and other conditions, and should be set with a nephrologist or renal dietitian rather than adopted from a general list.
