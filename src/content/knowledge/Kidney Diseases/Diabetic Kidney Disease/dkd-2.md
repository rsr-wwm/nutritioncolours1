## Overview

Treatment for diabetic kidney disease has expanded significantly beyond blood sugar and blood pressure control alone. ACE inhibitors or ARBs remain foundational for patients with albuminuria. SGLT2 inhibitors, originally diabetes medications, have been shown in major trials to slow DKD progression and reduce cardiovascular and kidney-failure risk, and are now recommended by nephrology and diabetes guidelines for eligible patients with DKD. GLP-1 receptor agonists have also shown kidney-protective benefits in trials and are increasingly used in this population, often alongside their effects on blood sugar and weight.

## Nutritional considerations

These medication classes interact with nutrition in a few practical ways: SGLT2 inhibitors work partly by causing glucose (and some calories) to be excreted in urine, and are associated with a modest increased risk of genital yeast infections and, rarely, a serious complication called diabetic ketoacidosis — patients starting these drugs should discuss hydration and sick-day guidance with their prescriber. GLP-1 receptor agonists commonly reduce appetite and can cause nausea, which may require smaller, more frequent meals during medication titration to maintain adequate nutrition. As with other DKD care, potassium-conscious eating remains relevant for patients also on ACE inhibitors/ARBs, based on lab monitoring.

## Safety note

Do not start, stop, or adjust doses of diabetes or kidney medications, or make major diet changes around a new prescription, without guidance from the prescribing clinician — appropriate precautions (like sick-day rules for SGLT2 inhibitors) depend on the specific drug and a patient's individual health status.
