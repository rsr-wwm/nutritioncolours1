## Overview

Long-standing or poorly controlled inflammatory bowel disease, especially Crohn's disease, can lead to structural complications such as strictures (narrowed, scarred segments of bowel) and fistulas (abnormal connections between the bowel and other organs or the skin). Chronic colonic inflammation — particularly in ulcerative colitis and Crohn's colitis affecting the colon — also raises long-term colorectal cancer risk, which is why patients with extensive, long-duration colitis are placed on a surveillance colonoscopy schedule by their gastroenterologist.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

- With strictures, a low-fiber or low-residue diet (avoiding tough, fibrous, or high-roughage foods such as raw vegetable skins, nuts, seeds, and popcorn) can reduce the risk of bowel obstruction. This is a targeted, temporary approach rather than a general IBD diet.
- Smaller, well-chewed meals and steady hydration reduce mechanical strain on narrowed bowel segments.
- Patients with fistulas or recovering from bowel surgery may need individualized nutrition support, sometimes including enteral or parenteral nutrition, guided by a dietitian.
- A nutrient-dense, balanced diet — once inflammation is controlled — supports mucosal healing and overall resilience, complementing medical management aimed at reducing long-term cancer risk.

## Safety Note

Strictures, fistulas, and cancer-risk surveillance require ongoing gastroenterology (and sometimes colorectal surgery) management. Report new abdominal pain, vomiting, inability to pass stool or gas, or rectal bleeding promptly. Any dietary changes for a stricture or fistula should be made in consultation with your care team.
