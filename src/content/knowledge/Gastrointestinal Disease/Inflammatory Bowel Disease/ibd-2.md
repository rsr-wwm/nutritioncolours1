## Overview

Biologic medications — including anti-TNF agents such as infliximab and adalimumab, and anti-integrin therapy such as vedolizumab — are used to induce and maintain remission in moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis by targeting specific immune pathways that drive chronic gut inflammation. These medications are prescribed, dosed, and monitored by a gastroenterologist, often alongside other therapies.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

- Adequate protein and calorie intake support tissue repair and immune function during biologic therapy, particularly for patients recovering from a flare or surgery.
- Correcting micronutrient deficiencies common in IBD — iron, vitamin B12, and vitamin D — supports overall treatment response and energy levels; levels are typically checked via routine blood tests.
- No specific food is known to interfere with how biologic drugs work, but general gut-supportive habits — balanced meals, fiber as tolerated, and limiting ultra-processed foods — are reasonable during maintenance therapy.
- Because biologics are immunosuppressive, following standard food-safety practices (thorough cooking, safe food handling, avoiding unpasteurized products) helps reduce infection risk.

## Safety Note

Decisions about starting, dosing, or monitoring biologic therapy — including infection screening and vaccination status — must be managed by a gastroenterologist. Nutrition can support recovery and general health but has no role in adjusting biologic treatment.
