## Overview

Peptic ulcers are sores in the lining of the stomach or upper small intestine (duodenum). The two dominant causes are infection with *Helicobacter pylori* bacteria and regular use of NSAID pain relievers (ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin) — contrary to older belief, stress and spicy food do not cause ulcers, though they can worsen symptoms in some people. H. pylori infection is diagnosed via breath, stool, or biopsy testing and treated with a course of antibiotics plus acid-suppressing medication.

## Dietary considerations

There is no specific "ulcer diet" required for healing — healing depends on treating the underlying cause (H. pylori eradication or stopping/reducing NSAID use) plus acid-suppressing medication (PPIs or H2 blockers) prescribed by a doctor. That said, individuals often find it helpful to identify and limit personal trigger foods (commonly alcohol, caffeine, or very spicy/fatty foods) that worsen their symptoms, and eating smaller, more frequent meals can ease discomfort during healing.

## Safety — warning signs

Seek immediate medical care for signs of a bleeding ulcer: black or tarry stools, vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds, or sudden severe abdominal pain — these can indicate serious complications requiring urgent endoscopic or surgical treatment.
