## Overview

A colonoscopy is a procedure in which a doctor uses a thin, flexible, lighted tube with a camera to examine the entire colon (large intestine) and rectum. It's used to investigate GI symptoms, to screen for colorectal cancer, and to remove polyps (small growths) if found, which can then be sent for testing.

## Bowel prep diet notes

A clean colon is essential for a clear view, so patients are typically asked to follow a low-fiber diet for a few days and then a clear-liquid diet for a day or so before the procedure, along with a prescribed laxative "bowel prep." Clear liquids generally means broth, plain gelatin, and clear juices — avoiding red or purple coloring and any solid food. Exact timing and restrictions vary by provider and bowel-prep product, so patients should follow their own doctor's written instructions rather than a generic diet.

## Safety

Inadequate bowel prep is one of the most common reasons a colonoscopy has to be repeated, so following the prep instructions precisely matters. Any questions about the prep diet, medication adjustments, or the procedure itself should go to the ordering physician's office.
