## Overview

Upper GI endoscopy (esophagogastroduodenoscopy, or EGD) is a procedure in which a doctor passes a thin, flexible, lighted tube with a camera through the mouth to examine the esophagus, stomach, and the first part of the small intestine (the duodenum). It's used to investigate symptoms like persistent heartburn, difficulty swallowing, or GI bleeding, and can also be used to take biopsies or treat certain findings during the same procedure.

## Prep-diet notes

Patients are typically asked not to eat or drink anything for up to about 8 hours before the procedure, so the stomach is empty enough for a clear view and to reduce risk during sedation. Exact fasting instructions vary by provider and should always follow what your doctor's office specifies.

## Safety

The procedure itself, the sedation approach, and any biopsy or treatment decisions are made by the endoscopy team. This is general background information, not a substitute for your provider's pre-procedure instructions.
