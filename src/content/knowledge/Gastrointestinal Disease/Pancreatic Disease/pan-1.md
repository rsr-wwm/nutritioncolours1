## Overview

Acute pancreatitis is sudden inflammation of the pancreas, most commonly caused by gallstones blocking the pancreatic duct or by heavy alcohol use; other causes include very high triglyceride levels and certain medications. Severity is assessed using clinical scoring systems such as Ranson's criteria, which help predict complications and guide the level of hospital care needed.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

- During an acute attack, patients are typically managed in the hospital and advanced to oral intake — usually starting with a low-fat diet — as pain and inflammation improve; prolonged fasting is generally avoided in favor of earlier feeding when the patient can tolerate it, under medical supervision.
- Long-term prevention depends on the underlying cause: a low-fat diet and weight management for gallstone-related pancreatitis, and complete alcohol avoidance when alcohol was a trigger.
- If elevated triglycerides contributed to the episode, limiting refined carbohydrates, alcohol, and saturated fat — combined with medical treatment — helps reduce recurrence risk.
- Smaller, more frequent low-fat meals are often better tolerated than large, fatty meals during recovery.

## Safety Note

Acute pancreatitis is a medical emergency requiring hospital evaluation and treatment. The dietary guidance above applies to recovery and long-term prevention, not to management of an active attack, which must be directed by a physician.
