## Overview

Chronic pancreatitis is long-term, progressive inflammation and scarring of the pancreas — often from repeated acute episodes, prolonged heavy alcohol use, or genetic factors — that can permanently impair the organ's ability to make digestive enzymes (exocrine pancreatic insufficiency) and, over time, insulin, leading to diabetes. Management combines pain control, enzyme replacement, and treatment of nutrient malabsorption and blood sugar changes.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

- A low-fat diet with smaller, more frequent meals is generally better tolerated and reduces the digestive workload on an impaired pancreas.
- Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT), taken with meals as prescribed, is central to managing exocrine pancreatic insufficiency and improving absorption of fat and other nutrients — dosing is set by a physician and should not be self-adjusted.
- Fat-soluble vitamin status (A, D, E, K) should be monitored and supplemented as needed, since malabsorption is common in chronic pancreatitis.
- If diabetes develops, carbohydrate awareness should be coordinated with a diabetes care plan, and alcohol should be avoided entirely.

## Safety Note

Nutrition management for chronic pancreatitis, including enzyme dosing and vitamin supplementation, should be individualized with a gastroenterologist and registered dietitian. Do not start, stop, or adjust enzyme therapy without medical guidance.
