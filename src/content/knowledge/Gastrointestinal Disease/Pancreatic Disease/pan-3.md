## Overview

Pancreatic cancer often causes few early symptoms, which is why it tends to be diagnosed at a later stage. Treatment may involve surgery — such as the Whipple procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy) for tumors in the head of the pancreas — chemotherapy, radiation, or a combination, depending on stage and location. Nutrition support is an important part of care throughout diagnosis, treatment, and recovery.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

- Pancreatic cancer and its treatment, especially after Whipple surgery, commonly cause exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, so pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy is frequently needed to help digest fat, protein, and carbohydrates.
- Small, frequent, protein- and calorie-dense meals help counter cancer- and treatment-related weight loss and appetite changes; oral nutrition supplements are often used when intake is inadequate.
- A low-fat approach with smaller meals is generally easier to tolerate after pancreatic surgery, alongside monitoring for new-onset diabetes.
- Working with an oncology dietitian is strongly recommended to individualize calorie, protein, and micronutrient needs throughout treatment.

## Safety Note

Nutrition support is an adjunct to, not a replacement for, surgery, chemotherapy, and oncologic care directed by a multidisciplinary cancer team. Unexplained weight loss, jaundice, or new abdominal or back pain warrant prompt medical evaluation.
