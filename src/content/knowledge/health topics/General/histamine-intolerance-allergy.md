# Histamine Intolerance Care

![Histamine Intolerance Care](/images/nutritioncolours-default.svg)

## Problem

Hives, nasal congestion, headaches, and bloating after consuming fermented or aged foods.

## Solution

Implement a low-histamine diet phase and support DAO enzyme cofactor pathways.

## Full Content

Histamine intolerance occurs when the body's capacity to break down histamine is overloaded. Under the clinical guidance of Dr. Shilpa Thakur at Nutrition Colours, we focus on a low-histamine diet phase, optimizing Diamine Oxidase (DAO) cofactors (Vitamin C, B6, Copper), and healing the gut barrier to reduce histamine absorption.

## Natural Approach

- Low histamine foods
- DAO cofactors (Vitamin C, B6)
- Quercetin support
- Fresh food sourcing

## FAQs

<details>
<summary>Why do healthy foods like spinach make me feel sick?</summary>
<p>Spinach, avocados, and fermented foods are naturally rich in histamines. If your DAO enzymes are sluggish, these foods trigger allergic reactions.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I eat leftovers?</summary>
<p>Histamine increases as food sits. Dr. Shilpa recommends consuming freshly prepared meals or freezing leftovers immediately at Nutrition Colours.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>What supplements support histamine clearance?</summary>
<p>Quercetin acts as a natural mast-cell stabilizer, while Vitamin C and B6 function as direct cofactors for the histamine-clearing DAO enzyme.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is histamine intolerance permanent?</summary>
<p>No, it is usually secondary to gut dysbiosis or leaky gut. Once the gut lining is restored, histamine tolerance returns to normal.</p>
</details>
