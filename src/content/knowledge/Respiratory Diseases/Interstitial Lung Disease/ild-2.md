## Overview

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is treated with antifibrotic medications — pirfenidone and nintedanib — that slow the rate of lung function decline, though they do not reverse existing scarring. For eligible patients with advanced disease, lung transplantation may be considered. Both antifibrotic therapy and transplant evaluation carry specific nutrition-related considerations.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Pirfenidone is best tolerated when taken with food, and patients are generally advised to avoid excessive sun exposure due to photosensitivity, which does not directly involve diet but is relevant to overall care; nintedanib commonly causes gastrointestinal side effects such as diarrhea, so adequate hydration and, if needed, temporary dietary adjustments (smaller meals, avoiding very high-fat foods during flares) can help manage tolerability. Patients being evaluated for lung transplant benefit from achieving and maintaining a healthy, disease-appropriate body weight and preserving muscle mass through adequate protein intake, since nutritional status affects surgical candidacy and post-transplant recovery. After transplant, patients typically follow specific dietary guidance from their transplant team related to immunosuppressant medications, including food-safety precautions to reduce infection risk.

## Safety Note

Nutrition supports tolerability of antifibrotic therapy and transplant readiness but does not replace these prescribed treatments. Any gastrointestinal side effects from pirfenidone or nintedanib, or questions about transplant-related dietary restrictions, should be directed to the treating pulmonology or transplant team.
