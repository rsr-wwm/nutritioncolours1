## Overview

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) encompasses a group of conditions, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), non-specific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), and connective tissue disease-associated ILD, that cause progressive scarring (fibrosis) of the lung tissue. This scarring reduces lung elasticity and gas exchange capacity, leading to breathlessness and reduced exercise tolerance that typically worsens over time.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

The increased work of breathing in pulmonary fibrosis raises calorie needs, and unintentional weight loss and muscle wasting are common as the disease progresses, so adequate calorie and protein intake to help preserve muscle mass and function is an important nutritional goal. Smaller, more frequent meals can ease the breathlessness some patients feel from a full stomach limiting diaphragm movement. Patients with connective tissue disease-associated ILD (such as from scleroderma) may also have gastrointestinal involvement, including reflux or slowed motility, so an evaluation of swallowing and digestive symptoms alongside nutrition planning is often warranted. A general anti-inflammatory, nutrient-dense eating pattern supports overall health, though no specific diet has been shown to slow fibrosis progression.

## Safety Note

Nutrition is supportive and does not replace antifibrotic medications, oxygen therapy, or pulmonary rehabilitation prescribed by a pulmonologist. Significant weight loss or new swallowing difficulty should be evaluated promptly by the treating specialist and a registered dietitian.
