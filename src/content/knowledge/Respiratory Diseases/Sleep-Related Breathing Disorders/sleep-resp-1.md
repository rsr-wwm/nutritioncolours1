## Overview

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) occurs when the upper airway repeatedly collapses or narrows during sleep, causing pauses in breathing, fragmented sleep, and reduced oxygen levels. First-line treatment is continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), with oral appliances or surgery considered for select patients. Excess body weight, particularly around the neck and upper airway, is one of the strongest modifiable risk factors for OSA severity.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Weight management is one of the most evidence-supported dietary interventions for OSA: even modest weight loss (in the range of 5-10% of body weight) has been shown to reduce the frequency of breathing pauses and improve symptoms in patients who are overweight. A balanced, calorie-appropriate eating pattern emphasizing vegetables, whole grains, lean protein, and healthy fats, combined with regular physical activity, supports sustainable weight loss more effectively than restrictive fad diets. Limiting alcohol and sedatives, especially in the hours before bed, is important because these relax throat muscles and can worsen airway collapse during sleep. Avoiding large, heavy meals close to bedtime may also reduce nighttime reflux, which can compound airway irritation in some patients with OSA.

## Safety Note

Weight loss and dietary changes support OSA management but do not replace CPAP, oral appliance therapy, or surgical treatment when these are prescribed. Patients should not discontinue or reduce CPAP use based on weight loss progress without reassessment by a sleep medicine specialist.
