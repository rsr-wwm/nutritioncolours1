## Overview

Severe asthma that remains poorly controlled despite high-dose inhaled therapy may be treated with injectable biologics such as omalizumab (anti-IgE), mepolizumab or benralizumab (anti-IL-5 pathway), and dupilumab (anti-IL-4/IL-13), which target specific inflammatory pathways in allergic and eosinophilic asthma. These therapies are prescribed and monitored by a pulmonologist or allergist based on biomarkers like blood eosinophil count and IgE levels. Nutrition plays a supportive role in overall airway health during biologic treatment but does not replace or reduce the need for these medications.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Patients on biologic therapy benefit from the same anti-inflammatory dietary foundation recommended for asthma generally: plenty of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and omega-3-rich fish, with limited ultra-processed and high-saturated-fat foods. Because obesity is associated with a distinct, harder-to-treat asthma phenotype and can blunt response to therapy, weight management through balanced calorie intake and regular physical activity (as tolerated and cleared by the treating physician) is a worthwhile long-term goal for patients who are overweight. Adequate protein and micronutrient intake supports general immune and tissue health during ongoing biologic treatment. Patients should continue to identify and avoid any personal food or environmental triggers that worsen symptoms, and should not use dietary supplements as a substitute for prescribed biologic dosing.

## Safety Note

Biologic therapy for severe asthma requires ongoing specialist supervision, and dietary changes should complement rather than replace this treatment. Patients should tell their prescribing physician about any significant diet, supplement, or weight changes, especially if considering elimination diets or new supplements that could interact with therapy.

The pathophysiology of asth 2 is deeply interconnected with systemic metabolic pathways. When analyzing the cellular microenvironment, it is evident that targeted nutrient availability plays a crucial role in mitigating oxidative stress and inflammatory cascades. Furthermore, the bioavailability of specific micronutrients, such as crucial antioxidants, trace minerals, and essential fatty acids, must be carefully considered. Deficiencies in these key areas often exacerbate the underlying mechanisms of asth 2, leading to an increased frequency of acute exacerbations and a general decline in the patient's quality of life. By focusing on nutrient density and optimal absorption rates, practitioners can build a robust defense against the systemic effects of the disease.
