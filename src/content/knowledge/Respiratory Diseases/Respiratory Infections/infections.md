## Overview

Respiratory infections range widely in cause and severity — from the common cold and influenza (viral, usually self-limiting) to bacterial pneumonia and tuberculosis (which require antibiotic treatment). They share a common route of transmission (respiratory droplets) and a common set of symptoms in mild cases: cough, congestion, sore throat, and fatigue, though severity, duration, and treatment differ substantially by specific diagnosis. This page covers general nutrition principles relevant across common respiratory infections; specific conditions like influenza, tuberculosis, and pneumonia have their own dedicated considerations.

## Nutritional Considerations

A few evidence-informed nutrition principles apply broadly during respiratory infections. Adequate hydration is consistently useful, since fever and reduced fluid intake during illness raise dehydration risk, and fluids also help thin mucus secretions. Maintaining adequate protein and calorie intake — even in smaller, more frequent portions when appetite is low — supports the immune response and helps prevent unintended weight loss, which matters most in longer or more severe infections (like pneumonia or TB) rather than a typical short-lived cold. General diet quality (adequate fruits, vegetables, and protein) supports baseline immune function, but no specific food, supplement, or "immune-boosting" product has strong evidence for preventing or curing respiratory infections — claims to that effect should be treated with skepticism.

## Safety

Most mild respiratory infections resolve with rest, fluids, and time. However, warning signs — difficulty breathing, chest pain, high or persistent fever, confusion, or symptoms that improve and then suddenly worsen — warrant prompt medical evaluation, since they can indicate a more serious infection like pneumonia. Anyone with a chronic health condition, who is very young, elderly, or pregnant should have a lower threshold for seeking medical care with respiratory symptoms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Is there a single best diet for fighting off a respiratory infection?
**A1:** No single diet or food "fights off" infection. A generally balanced diet with adequate protein, fluids, fruits, and vegetables supports the immune system's normal function; there's no strong evidence for special immune-boosting diets curing infections faster.

### Q2: Why is hydration repeatedly emphasized for respiratory infections?
**A2:** Fever and illness-related reduced intake increase dehydration risk, and adequate fluids also help thin mucus and ease congestion — making hydration one of the most consistently useful supportive measures across respiratory infections.

### Q3: When does a respiratory infection need medical attention rather than home care?
**A3:** Difficulty breathing, chest pain, high or persistent fever, confusion, or symptoms that worsen after initially improving are signs to seek medical care rather than continuing home management.
