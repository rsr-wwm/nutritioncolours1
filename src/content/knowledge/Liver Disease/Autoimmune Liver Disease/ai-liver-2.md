## Overview

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is an autoimmune condition that destroys small bile ducts, marked by anti-mitochondrial antibodies and treated with ursodeoxycholic acid. Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) causes progressive scarring and stricturing of the bile ducts, diagnosed via MRCP, and carries an increased risk of cholangiocarcinoma; it frequently overlaps with inflammatory bowel disease. Both conditions can progress to cirrhosis and eventually require liver transplant evaluation.

## Nutritional & dietary considerations

- **Chronic cholestasis (reduced bile flow) impairs absorption of fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K**, so periodic deficiency screening and clinician-guided supplementation — not blanket high-dose self-supplementation — is often part of routine care.
- **Pruritus (itching)**, a common symptom in both conditions, is not caused by specific foods and is not managed through diet; it's addressed with medication and, in refractory cases, other targeted therapies.
- **If cirrhosis develops**, follow the cirrhosis-specific sodium and protein guidance covered elsewhere in this pillar.
- **PSC patients with coexisting inflammatory bowel disease** may need additional dietary accommodation for their bowel disease specifically, individualized with their gastroenterology team.
- **Alcohol should generally be limited or avoided** given the added burden on an already-compromised biliary/liver system.

## Safety note

Fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies in PBC/PSC should be identified through blood testing rather than assumed, since over-supplementing vitamin A or D without monitoring can itself cause harm. PSC's cholangiocarcinoma risk means new symptoms (jaundice, weight loss, worsening itching) warrant prompt evaluation.
