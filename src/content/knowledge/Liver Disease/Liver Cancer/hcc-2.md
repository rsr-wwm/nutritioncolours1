## Overview

Hepatocellular carcinoma treatment depends heavily on BCLC stage and underlying liver function. Early-stage disease may be treated with surgical resection or liver transplant (guided by the Milan criteria); small tumors can be treated with ablation techniques like radiofrequency ablation; intermediate-stage disease is often treated with transarterial chemoembolization (TACE); and advanced disease relies on systemic therapy such as sorafenib, lenvatinib, or the combination of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab.

## Nutritional & dietary considerations

- **Nutrition needs vary substantially by treatment type and underlying liver function** — pre- and post-surgical nutrition optimization, appetite and nausea management during systemic therapy, and adequate protein/calorie intake to limit treatment-related muscle loss are typically coordinated by an oncology or hepatology dietitian.
- **No supplement or diet replaces guideline-directed cancer treatment** for HCC; be cautious of unregulated "liver support" products marketed alongside a cancer diagnosis.
- **Patients with underlying cirrhosis need cirrhosis-appropriate nutrition** (adequate protein, individualized sodium restriction — see the Cirrhosis pillar) layered alongside cancer-directed nutrition care, since the two needs can otherwise conflict.
- **Fasting and dietary requirements around procedures** (resection, ablation, TACE) should come directly from the treating surgical/interventional team.

## Safety note

Treatment selection for HCC is a multidisciplinary decision based on tumor burden, liver function, and overall health, made by a hepatology/oncology team — this is not a condition to manage through diet alone. Report new appetite loss, jaundice, or confusion to the care team promptly.
