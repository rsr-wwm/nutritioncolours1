## Overview

Hepatitis A spreads through the fecal-oral route (contaminated food or water) and is usually self-limited in adults, with a highly effective vaccine available for prevention. Hepatitis E is typically waterborne and also usually self-limited, but it carries a notably higher risk of acute liver failure in pregnant women. Most cases of both resolve with supportive care alone; there's no specific antiviral treatment for the typical case. Prevention relies on vaccination (hepatitis A) and safe food/water practices, especially important for travelers to areas with limited sanitation infrastructure.

## Nutritional & dietary considerations

- **During acute illness, focus on hydration and easily tolerated foods**, since nausea, vomiting, and appetite loss are common; small, frequent, bland meals are often better tolerated than large ones.
- **There is no special "detox" or cleansing diet that speeds recovery** — the liver clears the infection on its own timeline in typical self-limited cases.
- **Alcohol should be avoided until liver enzymes have normalized**, as confirmed by follow-up blood work with your clinician.
- **Most people recover fully without any long-term dietary restriction** once the acute illness resolves.
- **Travelers to higher-risk areas** benefit more from safe water/food practices and vaccination than from any dietary intervention during travel.

## Safety note

Hepatitis E in pregnancy can be severe and requires close medical monitoring — this is not a situation for home management. Anyone with jaundice, confusion, or worsening symptoms during acute viral hepatitis should seek prompt medical evaluation, since a small number of cases can progress to acute liver failure.
