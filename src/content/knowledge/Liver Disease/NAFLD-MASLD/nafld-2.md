## Overview

Treatment for NASH/MASH (steatohepatitis with inflammation and fibrosis risk) centers on weight loss and exercise, with pharmacotherapy layered in for patients who don't achieve enough improvement through lifestyle change alone or who have more advanced disease. Options include GLP-1 receptor agonists (originally developed for diabetes/weight management), resmetirom (a thyroid hormone receptor-beta agonist approved for NASH with fibrosis), pioglitazone, vitamin E in select patients, and bariatric surgery for eligible candidates with severe obesity.

## Nutritional & dietary considerations

- **Weight loss of roughly 7–10% of body weight** remains the therapeutic target most strongly associated with histologic improvement in NASH, achieved through a sustained calorie deficit, reduced added sugar, and a Mediterranean-style eating pattern.
- **Vitamin E** (800 IU/day) has evidence for improving liver histology in non-diabetic adults with biopsy-proven NASH per AASLD guidance, but it carries potential risks at that dose (including bleeding risk and a signal for prostate cancer in some studies) and should only be used under a clinician's direction — not self-started.
- **Alcohol avoidance** supports any pharmacologic or lifestyle intervention; continued drinking undermines treatment.
- **Regular exercise** (aerobic and resistance) improves insulin sensitivity and supports weight maintenance alongside any medication.
- Diet and lifestyle changes are complementary to, not a substitute for, prescribed medications like GLP-1 agonists or resmetirom, which require medical supervision and monitoring.

## Safety note

Emerging drug therapies for NASH have specific eligibility criteria, dosing, and monitoring requirements set by a treating physician. Nutrition changes support these treatments but should be coordinated with — not substituted for — medical care, particularly for patients with existing fibrosis or cirrhosis.
