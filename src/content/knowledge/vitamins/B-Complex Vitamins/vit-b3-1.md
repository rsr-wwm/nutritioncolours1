## Overview

*Also known as: niacin.*

Niacin forms the coenzymes NAD and NADP, which are essential to energy production, DNA repair, and cellular signalling.

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Men 19+ | 16 mg NE |
| Women 19+ | 14 mg NE |
| Pregnancy | 18 mg NE |
| Lactation | 17 mg NE |

**Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 35 mg/day for adults — this UL applies to niacin from **supplements and fortification**, not from food, and is based on skin flushing.

## Food sources

Meat, poultry, fish, peanuts, legumes, and enriched/fortified grains. The body can also make some niacin from the amino acid tryptophan.

## Deficiency

Deficiency causes pellagra — classically the 'three Ds': dermatitis, diarrhoea, and dementia.

## Too much

High-dose nicotinic acid (used to treat cholesterol) causes skin flushing and, at very high doses, liver injury — hence the supplemental UL.
