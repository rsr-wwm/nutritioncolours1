## Overview

*Also known as: retinol / carotenoids.*

Vitamin A is essential for vision (especially night vision), immune function, reproduction, and normal growth and cell differentiation. It exists as preformed vitamin A (retinol, from animal foods) and provitamin-A carotenoids (like beta-carotene, from plants).

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Men 19+ | 900 mcg RAE |
| Women 19+ | 700 mcg RAE |
| Pregnancy | 770 mcg RAE |
| Lactation | 1,300 mcg RAE |

**Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 3,000 mcg RAE/day for adults — this applies to **preformed** vitamin A (retinol) only. Provitamin-A carotenoids like beta-carotene are not toxic at high dietary intakes.

## Food sources

Preformed (retinol): liver, fish oils, dairy, and eggs. Provitamin-A carotenoids: orange and yellow vegetables (carrots, sweet potato, pumpkin), and dark leafy greens.

## Deficiency

Deficiency causes night blindness and xerophthalmia — the leading cause of preventable childhood blindness worldwide — plus impaired immunity.

## Too much

Chronic excess of preformed vitamin A (usually from supplements or liver) is toxic: it can damage the liver, weaken bones, and is teratogenic (causes birth defects) in pregnancy.
