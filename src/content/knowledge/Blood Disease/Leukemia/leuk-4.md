## Overview

Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is caused by a genetic abnormality — the Philadelphia chromosome — that fuses the BCR and ABL1 genes, producing an abnormal protein that drives uncontrolled growth of myeloid cells. The discovery of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), starting with imatinib and followed by second- and third-generation drugs (dasatinib, nilotinib, ponatinib), transformed CML from a rapidly fatal disease into one that many patients manage long-term with a daily pill and regular monitoring of BCR-ABL1 levels by PCR testing. Some patients who achieve a deep, sustained molecular response may be eligible to attempt treatment-free remission under close medical supervision.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Grapefruit and CYP3A4 interactions**: Grapefruit, grapefruit juice, and Seville (sour) oranges inhibit an enzyme (CYP3A4) that metabolizes many TKIs, which can raise drug levels and increase side effects. Most patients on imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, or ponatinib are advised to avoid these unless their care team says otherwise.
- **Supplement and herb interactions**: St. John's Wort induces the same enzyme system and can lower TKI blood levels enough to reduce effectiveness; it should be avoided during TKI treatment. Any new supplement should be reviewed with an oncology pharmacist.
- **QT interval considerations**: Some TKIs (notably nilotinib) can affect heart rhythm; maintaining normal electrolyte levels (potassium, magnesium) and avoiding other QT-prolonging substances is something your care team may monitor.
- **General nutrition**: A balanced diet supports overall well-being during long-term TKI therapy; gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, diarrhea) are common with some TKIs and may respond to smaller, more frequent meals.

## Safety & When to See a Doctor

CML requires ongoing monitoring by a hematologist-oncologist, including regular PCR testing even when doing well on treatment. Never stop or adjust a TKI without medical guidance, and always disclose new medications, supplements, or dietary changes (especially grapefruit products) to your care team.
