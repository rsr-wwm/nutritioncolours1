## Overview

Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) is a fast-growing cancer of the bone marrow's lymphoid cell line, most common in children but also occurring in adults, where it tends to behave more aggressively. It's classified by whether it arises from B-cells or T-cells, and by specific genetic features such as the Philadelphia chromosome (BCR-ABL1), which changes treatment choice. Treatment typically involves multi-phase chemotherapy protocols, often combined with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor for Philadelphia-chromosome-positive disease, and newer immunotherapies such as blinatumomab or CAR-T cell therapy for relapsed or refractory disease. Because leukemia cells can involve the central nervous system, CNS-directed therapy is often part of treatment.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Neutropenic food safety**: As with other leukemias, chemotherapy causes periods of low white blood cell counts. Food-safety precautions (thoroughly cooked foods, avoiding unpasteurized products, careful produce washing) are commonly advised during these windows, per your treatment center's protocol.
- **Growth and development in children**: Pediatric patients have ongoing nutritional needs for growth; oncology dietitians typically monitor weight, height, and intake closely throughout treatment, and steroid components of chemotherapy can increase appetite and blood sugar.
- **Managing treatment side effects**: Nausea, mucositis, and taste changes are common; softer, milder foods and smaller, more frequent meals are often better tolerated.
- **CAR-T and cytokine release syndrome**: Patients undergoing CAR-T therapy are closely monitored in a hospital setting; nutrition support during this period is managed directly by the treatment team.

## Safety & When to See a Doctor

ALL is a medical emergency requiring prompt treatment by a hematologist-oncologist, often at a specialized center. Fever during periods of low white blood cell counts requires immediate medical attention. All nutrition adjustments during active treatment should be made in coordination with the oncology team.
