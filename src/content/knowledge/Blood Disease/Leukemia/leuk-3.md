## Overview

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is a slow-growing cancer of mature B-lymphocytes, most often diagnosed in older adults, sometimes incidentally on routine blood work. Because it can progress very slowly, many patients with early-stage, asymptomatic disease are managed with "watchful waiting" — regular monitoring without immediate treatment — using staging systems (Rai and Binet) and markers like FISH cytogenetics to help predict behavior. When treatment is needed, options include targeted oral therapies such as BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib), venetoclax, and antibody therapies, which have largely replaced traditional chemotherapy as first-line treatment for many patients.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Drug interactions with BTK inhibitors**: Grapefruit, grapefruit juice, and Seville oranges can raise blood levels of ibrutinib and some other BTK inhibitors by interfering with their metabolism, and St. John's Wort can lower drug levels and reduce effectiveness. Check with your oncology pharmacist about any food or supplement before adding it to your routine while on these medications.
- **Infection risk**: CLL itself, independent of treatment, can impair normal immune function, so general food-safety practices (thorough cooking, hand hygiene, careful handling of raw foods) are reasonable, and your care team may give more specific guidance during active treatment.
- **General nutrition**: For patients on watchful waiting, there's no specific "CLL diet"; a balanced, nutrient-dense diet supporting overall health and immune function is reasonable.
- **Weight and metabolic health**: Maintaining a healthy weight and cardiovascular health is relevant, as some CLL therapies carry cardiac risks that are monitored by the care team.

## Safety & When to See a Doctor

CLL should be monitored by a hematologist-oncologist even when no treatment is planned. Report fevers, drenching night sweats, unexplained weight loss, or rapidly enlarging lymph nodes promptly. Discuss any new supplement, herbal product, or grapefruit-containing food with your care team if you are taking a BTK inhibitor or other targeted therapy.
