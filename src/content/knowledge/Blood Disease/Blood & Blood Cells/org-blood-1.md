## Overview

Blood is made up of plasma (the liquid portion, mostly water, along with proteins, electrolytes, and nutrients) and formed elements suspended in it: red blood cells (erythrocytes), white blood cells (leukocytes), and platelets (thrombocytes). Red blood cells carry oxygen using hemoglobin and are continuously produced in the bone marrow. White blood cells are the immune system's front line, including neutrophils that fight bacterial infection and lymphocytes involved in immune memory. Platelets, together with clotting proteins in plasma, are responsible for hemostasis — stopping bleeding by forming a plug at a site of injury and then a stable fibrin clot.

## Nutritional Considerations

Producing and maintaining healthy blood cells depends on an adequate supply of specific nutrients:

- **Iron, vitamin B12, and folate** are essential for red blood cell production; shortfalls in any of these can lead to anemia.
- **Protein** provides the building blocks for hemoglobin and other blood proteins, including clotting factors and immune proteins (antibodies).
- **Vitamin K** is required by the liver to produce several clotting factors (including factors II, VII, IX, and X); it is found in leafy green vegetables and is also produced by gut bacteria.
- **Vitamin C** supports the absorption of non-heme (plant-based) iron and contributes to overall tissue and vascular health.
- **Copper and vitamin A** also play supporting roles in normal red blood cell formation, though deficiencies of these are less common than iron, B12, or folate deficiency.

## Safety & When to See a Doctor

This is general physiology information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. If you notice symptoms such as persistent fatigue, unusual bruising or bleeding, frequent infections, or unexplained pallor, a doctor can order blood tests (such as a complete blood count) to check for an underlying blood disorder.
