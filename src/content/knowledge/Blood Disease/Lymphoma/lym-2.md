## Overview

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) is an umbrella term for a diverse group of cancers arising from lymphocytes, with very different behaviors and treatments. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common aggressive subtype, typically treated with R-CHOP chemoimmunotherapy. Follicular lymphoma is usually slow-growing and, depending on symptoms and disease burden, may be managed with watchful waiting or rituximab-based treatment. Mantle cell lymphoma is often treated with BTK inhibitors such as ibrutinib alongside other regimens. Burkitt lymphoma is a rare but very fast-growing subtype requiring intensive treatment, and marginal zone lymphoma is generally slow-growing. CAR-T cell therapy is now an option for some patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **During chemoimmunotherapy (e.g., R-CHOP)**: Nausea, mucositis, and appetite changes are common; smaller, more frequent, bland meals are often better tolerated, and the steroid component (prednisone) can temporarily raise blood sugar and appetite.
- **Neutropenic food safety**: Chemotherapy regimens can lower white blood cell counts; follow your treatment center's specific food-safety guidance (thorough cooking, avoiding unpasteurized products, careful produce handling) during these periods.
- **BTK inhibitor interactions**: For mantle cell lymphoma patients on ibrutinib or similar drugs, grapefruit and grapefruit juice can raise drug levels, and St. John's Wort can lower them — both should be avoided or discussed with your oncology pharmacist.
- **Watchful waiting (follicular, marginal zone lymphoma)**: There is no specific curative diet; a balanced, nutrient-dense diet supporting general health is reasonable while under observation.
- **CAR-T therapy**: Patients undergoing CAR-T cell therapy are closely monitored in a hospital or specialized outpatient setting, with nutrition support managed directly by that team.

## Safety & When to See a Doctor

Report fevers during periods of low white blood cell counts immediately, as well as any new or worsening lymph node swelling, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss. Treatment decisions for non-Hodgkin lymphoma should be made with a hematologist-oncologist; this content is educational and does not replace individualized care.
