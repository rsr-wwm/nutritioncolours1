## Overview

Palliative care focuses on relieving symptoms and improving quality of life for patients with serious illness, and can be provided alongside curative or life-prolonging treatment at any stage of cancer. Hospice care is a form of palliative care focused on comfort in the final phase of life, typically when treatment aimed at curing or controlling the cancer is no longer pursued. Advance directives allow patients to document their care preferences in advance.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Appetite loss and cachexia**: Reduced appetite and unintentional weight loss are common in advanced cancer and are often part of the underlying disease process rather than something that can be reversed by eating more. This distinction is important for both patients and families.
- **Comfort-focused eating**: In palliative and hospice settings, the goal typically shifts from maximizing calorie intake to supporting comfort and quality of life — offering small amounts of preferred foods, without pressure to finish meals.
- **Family and caregiver guidance**: It can be distressing for families to watch a loved one eat less. Care teams often provide reassurance that reduced intake is a natural part of advanced illness and that gentle offering of food, rather than insistence, is usually more comfortable for the patient.
- **Appetite stimulants and artificial nutrition**: Medications to stimulate appetite, and decisions about tube feeding or intravenous nutrition, are individualized medical decisions made with the palliative care team based on the patient's goals, prognosis, and wishes — not routine interventions.
- **Symptom management**: Nausea, dry mouth, and swallowing difficulties are common in advanced illness; the palliative care team can offer texture modifications, mouth care, and medications to improve comfort around eating.

> **Important:** Nutrition guidance in palliative and hospice care should always be individualized around the patient's goals and comfort, in partnership with the palliative care team, oncologist, and a registered dietitian where available. This information is not a substitute for care planning with your medical team.
