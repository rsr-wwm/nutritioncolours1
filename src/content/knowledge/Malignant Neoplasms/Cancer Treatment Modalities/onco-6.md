## Overview

Targeted therapy uses drugs designed to act on specific molecular changes that drive cancer growth, rather than broadly affecting all dividing cells. Examples include tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) that block specific growth signals, monoclonal antibodies that target proteins on cancer cells, and PARP inhibitors used in cancers with certain DNA-repair mutations (such as BRCA-related cancers). Because these drugs target specific pathways, side-effect profiles vary by agent.

## Nutritional Considerations During Treatment

- **Gastrointestinal side effects**: Diarrhea, nausea, and mouth sores are common with many TKIs; smaller, more frequent meals and adequate fluid intake can help manage symptoms, alongside any medications the care team prescribes.
- **Food-drug interactions**: Some targeted agents interact with grapefruit, grapefruit juice, or certain other foods that affect drug metabolism. Patients should ask their oncologist or pharmacist whether their specific medication has known food interactions, since this varies by drug.
- **Liver function monitoring**: Some targeted therapies require periodic liver function tests; any diet changes related to liver findings should be individualized by the care team.
- **Appetite and taste changes**: These can occur with several targeted agents; working with a dietitian to find tolerable, nutrient-dense foods can help maintain intake.
- **Maintaining nutrition status**: Because many targeted therapies are taken over long periods (sometimes months to years), consistent attention to protein, calorie, and micronutrient intake supports the ability to stay on treatment as prescribed.

> **Important:** Nutrition strategies described here are supportive and adjunct to oncology-directed treatment. They do not replace targeted therapy prescribed by your care team, and dietary changes should never be used to substitute for or delay prescribed treatment. Always consult your treating oncologist, pharmacist, and a registered oncology dietitian before making dietary changes.
