## Overview

Immunotherapy uses the body's own immune system to recognize and attack cancer cells. Approaches include immune checkpoint inhibitors (which block proteins that prevent immune cells from attacking cancer), CAR-T cell therapy (in which a patient's T cells are engineered to target cancer cells), and cancer vaccines. These treatments work differently from chemotherapy and can cause a distinct set of side effects, known as immune-related adverse events, because the activated immune system can also affect healthy tissue.

## Nutritional Considerations During Treatment

- **Gastrointestinal side effects**: Checkpoint inhibitors can cause immune-related colitis, leading to diarrhea or abdominal pain. Any new or persistent GI symptoms should be reported to the care team promptly, since they may need medical management rather than dietary self-treatment; a low-fiber or low-residue diet may be recommended temporarily during a flare, at the care team's direction.
- **Endocrine side effects**: Immunotherapy can affect the thyroid, adrenal glands, or pancreas, which may influence appetite, energy levels, and blood sugar. Routine lab monitoring is used to catch these changes early.
- **Liver-related side effects**: Immune-related hepatitis can occur; liver function is typically monitored with blood tests, and any dietary adjustments should be guided by the care team.
- **CAR-T cell therapy**: This is usually preceded by lymphodepleting chemotherapy and can involve a hospital stay for monitoring of cytokine release syndrome. Nutrition support during this period is typically managed closely by the inpatient care team.
- **General intake**: Maintaining adequate protein and calories supports recovery and helps the body tolerate treatment; fatigue from treatment can reduce appetite, so smaller, more frequent meals may help.

> **Important:** Nutrition strategies described here are supportive and adjunct to oncology-directed treatment. They do not replace immunotherapy or other treatments prescribed by your care team. Report new or worsening symptoms to your oncology team promptly, and consult a registered oncology dietitian for individualized guidance.
