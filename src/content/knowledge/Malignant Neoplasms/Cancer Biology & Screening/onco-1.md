## Overview

Carcinogenesis is the multistep process by which normal cells accumulate genetic and epigenetic changes that lead to uncontrolled growth and, eventually, invasive cancer. Tumor markers are substances — often proteins — found in blood, urine, or tissue that can be elevated in the presence of certain cancers; they are used to help support diagnosis, monitor treatment response, or watch for recurrence, but are generally not used alone to diagnose cancer. TNM staging is a standardized system that describes a cancer's extent based on Tumor size, lymph Node involvement, and Metastasis, and is used to guide treatment decisions and estimate prognosis.

## Nutrition's Role in Cancer Care

- **Nutrition screening at diagnosis**: Assessing weight history, appetite, and muscle mass is a routine part of oncology workups, since unintentional weight loss and muscle loss (sarcopenia) at diagnosis can affect treatment tolerance and are factors clinicians track alongside staging information.
- **Ongoing monitoring**: Because treatment can affect appetite and intake, many cancer centers reassess nutrition status throughout treatment, not only at diagnosis.
- **Individualized referral**: Patients with significant weight loss or eating difficulties at diagnosis are often referred early to a registered oncology dietitian, which can help support treatment tolerance.

## Diet and Cancer Risk

No diet or supplement has been shown to control the biological process of carcinogenesis once it has begun. For risk reduction broadly, the World Cancer Research Fund (WCRF/AICR) recommends maintaining a healthy body weight, staying physically active, and eating a diet rich in whole grains, vegetables, fruits, and legumes while limiting red and processed meat, alcohol, and sugary drinks.

> **Important:** This information is educational and does not replace diagnostic evaluation, staging, or treatment planning by your oncology team. Always consult your treating oncologist and, where appropriate, a registered oncology dietitian.
