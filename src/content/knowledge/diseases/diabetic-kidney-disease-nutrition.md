## Clinical Definition & Overview

**Diabetic Kidney Disease (DKD)**, jise diabetic nephropathy bhi kehte hain, diabetes ke chronic hyperglycemia ke karan kidneys ki filtering ability dhire‑dhire degrade hoti hai. It is a leading cause of CKD worldwide. Controlling blood glucose, blood pressure, and adopting a kidney‑friendly diet are essential to slow progression and reduce cardiovascular risk.

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **Low‑Glycemic, High‑Fiber Carbohydrates:** Whole oats, barley, brown rice (in controlled portions), quinoa, and legumes (if [potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1) is permitted).
- **Plant‑Based, Moderate‑Protein Sources:** Tofu, tempeh, egg whites, skinless poultry, and fish – aim for 0.8 g/kg body weight per day, adjusted for kidney function.
- **Low‑Potassium Fruits & Vegetables:** Berries, apples, cauliflower, cabbage, bell peppers, green beans.
- **Healthy Fats:** Olive oil, canola oil, avocado (limited due to potassium), nuts/seeds (small portions for calorie density).
- **Renal‑Specific Multivitamins:** B‑complex and Vitamin C formulations without excessive potassium or [phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1).

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑Sugar Foods & Beverages:** Sodas, candy, pastries, sweetened fruit juices – spikes blood glucose.
- **High‑Potassium Items (advanced DKD):** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes.
- **High‑Phosphorus Foods:** Dairy, nuts, seeds, processed meats, cola drinks.
- **High‑[Sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) Processed Foods:** Packaged snacks, canned soups, fast food, pickles.
- **Excessive Red Meat & Protein Supplements:** Can increase uremic toxin load.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Consistent Carbohydrate Distribution:** 3‑4 meals with balanced carbs to avoid glucose spikes.
- **Portion Control:** Use measuring cups for protein and high‑potassium foods; follow plate method (½ veggies, ¼ protein, ¼ carbs).
- **Fluid Management:** Follow physician’s fluid limit; sip water throughout the day, use ice chips if needed.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Blood Sugar Monitoring:** Pair meals with glucose checks; adjust carbohydrate intake accordingly.
- **Blood Pressure Control:** Incorporate DASH diet principles – low sodium, high fiber.
- **Stage‑Specific Adjustments:** Reduce potassium & phosphorus as eGFR declines; increase protein in dialysis‑dependent stages.

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medical Therapy (ACE inhibitors/ARBs, SGLT2 inhibitors, insulin, antihypertensives)** | Slows kidney decline, improves glycemic control, reduces proteinuria. | Medication side effects, pill burden, cost.
| **Nutritional Management (DKD‑specific diet)** | Improves glycemic stability, reduces kidney workload, supports cardiovascular health. | Requires strict adherence, monitoring of electrolytes and protein.
| **Combined Approach** | Maximizes renal protection and metabolic control. | Higher coordination effort between nephrologist, endocrinologist, and dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

<details><summary>1. DKD ka primary cause kya hota hai?</summary>Chronic hyperglycemia leading to glomerular damage and increased renal workload.
</details>
<details><summary>2. Diabetes aur kidney disease me diet kaise link hoti hai?</summary>High glucose increases filtration pressure; a low‑glycemic diet reduces this stress.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Protein intake ko kaise balance karen?</summary>Typically 0.8 g/kg body weight; may increase to 1.0 g/kg in dialysis.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Potassium restriction kab shuru karen?</summary>When eGFR falls below 45 mL/min/1.73 m², potassium monitoring becomes crucial.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Phosphorus ko kaise limit karen?</summary>Avoid dairy, nuts, processed meats, read labels for phosphate additives.
</details>
<details><summary>6. Sodium intake kitna hona chahiye?</summary>Aim for < 2300 mg per day (≈1 tsp salt) or less if hypertension is uncontrolled.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Kya low‑fat dairy allowed hai?</summary>Usually limited due to phosphorus; fortified low‑phosphorus alternatives (almond milk) are preferred.
</details>
<details><summary>8. How often should blood glucose be checked with meals?</summary>Before and 1‑2 hours after meals to gauge post‑prandial spikes.
</details>
<details><summary>9. Can I still eat fruit?</summary>Yes, choose low‑potassium fruits and limit portion size (½ cup per serving).
</details>
<details><summary>10. Is fasting safe for DKD?</summary>Generally not recommended without medical supervision as it can cause hypoglycemia.
</details>
<details><summary>11. What fluids are safest?</summary>Water, unsweetened herbal teas, and limited low‑potassium juice (e.g., cranberry).
</details>
<details><summary>12. How does blood pressure affect DKD?</summary>Hypertension accelerates kidney damage; diet and antihypertensives are crucial.
</details>
<details><summary>13. Can I take over‑the‑counter supplements?</summary>Only renal‑approved multivitamins; many OTC contain excess potassium/phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>14. When should I see a renal dietitian?</summary>At diagnosis, when eGFR drops below 60, or when dietary changes become complex.
</details>
<details><summary>15. How often need lab tests?</summary>Every 3‑6 months for eGFR, electrolytes, HbA1c, and lipid profile.
</details>
