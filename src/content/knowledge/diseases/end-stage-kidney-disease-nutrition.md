## Clinical Definition & Overview

**End‑Stage Kidney Disease (ESKD)**, also called kidney failure, tabah ho gayi hai jab eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m² hota hai. At this point, kidneys cannot sustain life without renal replacement therapy (hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or transplant). Nutrition becomes critical to manage uremic symptoms, maintain muscle mass, and prepare for possible transplantation.

---

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **High‑Quality Protein (dialysis‑adjusted):** Egg whites, fish, chicken, lean meat – 1.2‑1.3 g/kg body weight/day for dialysis patients.
- **Calorie‑Dense Carbohydrates:** White rice, pasta, breads, mashed potatoes (if [potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1)‑controlled).
- **Healthy Fats:** Olive oil, avocado (if potassium permitted), nut butter (small amounts).
- **Low‑Potassium Fruits & Veggies:** Apples, berries, cabbage, cauliflower, cucumber, bell peppers.
- **Renal‑Specific Supplements:** Renal‑appropriate multivitamins, iron, and vitamin D as prescribed.

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑Potassium Produce:** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, avocado.
- **High‑[Phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1) Items:** Dairy, nuts, seeds, colas, processed meats.
- **High‑[Sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) Processed Foods:** Canned soups, fast food, salty snacks.
- **Excessive Protein (non‑dialysis):** Red meat large portions.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Frequent Small Meals:** 5‑6 meals per day to meet calorie needs without overloading kidneys.
- **Portion Control:** Measure protein servings (≈ 3‑4 oz per meal) and count potassium‑rich foods.
- **Fluid Management:** Follow fluid prescription; if unrestricted, aim for 1‑1.5 L/day, otherwise use ice chips.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Dialysis‑Specific Adjustments:** Post‑dialysis meals may need extra protein and potassium.
- **Transplant Preparation:** Maintain good nutrition, adequate protein, and stable weight.
- **Phosphate Binders:** Take with meals if prescribed.

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dialysis (Hemodialysis/Peritoneal)** | Quickly removes uremic toxins, controls fluid/electrolytes.| Invasive, risk of infection, requires regular sessions.
| **Nutritional Management** | Supports muscle mass, reduces symptom burden, may improve transplant outcomes.| Highly restrictive, risk of malnutrition if not monitored.
| **Combined Approach** | Balances toxin removal with nutritional support for better quality of life.| Requires close coordination among nephrologist, dietitian, and patient.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

<details><summary>1. ESKD me protein intake kitna hona chahiye?</summary>Dialysis patients typically need 1.2‑1.3 g/kg/day; non‑dialysis patients may require restriction.
</details>
<details><summary>2. Kya main salt‑free diet follow kar sakta/ sakti hoon?</summary>Yes, low‑sodium diet helps control fluid overload and blood pressure.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Potassium restriction ki zaroorat kab shuru hoti hai?</summary>Usually when eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m² or on dialysis.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Fluid restriction kaise manage karun?</summary>Follow physician's limit; sip water, use ice chips, avoid sugary drinks.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Phosphate binders ko khane ke saath lena zaroori hai?</summary>Yes, they work best when taken with meals containing phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>6. Dialysis ke baad diet me kya change hota hai?</summary>Protein and potassium allowances often increase after a session.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Kya transplant ke liye weight gain zaroori hai?</summary>Maintaining a healthy weight (BMI 18‑25) is ideal; both under‑ and overweight can affect outcomes.
</details>
<details><summary>8. Kya alcohol allowed hai?</summary>Limited intake; consult nephrologist for safe limits.
</details>
<details><summary>9. [Calcium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/calcium/min-ca-1) supplements safe hain?</summary>Only renal‑approved calcium; excess can cause vascular calcification.
</details>
<details><summary>10. Kya vegetarian diet follow kar sakta/ sakti hoon?</summary>Possible, but ensure adequate protein and monitor potassium/phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>11. How often should I get labs?</summary>Typically monthly for dialysis patients; more frequent if unstable.
</details>
<details><summary>12. Kya supplementing with B‑complex vitamins helpful?</summary>Yes, especially B12 and folate, but use renal‑specific formulations.
</details>
<details><summary>13. Kya herbal teas safe hain?</summary>Most are safe in moderation; avoid those with high potassium.
</details>
<details><summary>14. Kya I should limit dairy completely?</summary>Low‑phosphorus alternatives (almond milk, rice milk) are preferred.
</details>
<details><summary>15. How to handle nausea during dialysis?</summary>Small, bland meals, ginger tea, and frequent light snacks can help.
</details>
