## Clinical Definition & Overview

**Hypertensive nephrosclerosis** ek chronic kidney disease hai jo prolonged high blood pressure ke karan kidney ke blood vessels ki thickening aur scarring se hoti hai. Yeh condition generally CKD stages 3‑4 mein manifest hoti hai, aur blood‑pressure control sabse important intervention hai.

---

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **DASH‑style foods:** Whole grains (oats, barley), low‑fat dairy alternatives, nuts (in moderation), and legumes (if [potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1) allows).
- **Lean proteins:** Skinless chicken, fish, egg whites –≈ 0.8 g/kg body weight per day.
- **Low‑potassium fruits & vegetables:** Apples, berries, cabbage, cauliflower, bell peppers, cucumber.
- **Healthy fats:** Olive oil, canola oil – support cardiovascular health.
- **Potassium‑controlled carbs:** White rice, white bread, pasta – easy on kidneys.

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑[sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) processed foods:** Packaged snacks, canned soups, fast food, salted nuts.
- **High‑potassium items (if eGFR < 45):** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes.
- **High‑[phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1) foods:** Dairy, nuts, seeds, colas, processed meats.
- **Excessive red meat:** Increases saturated fat and sodium.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Regular meals:** 3 main meals + 2 snacks to maintain stable blood pressure.
- **Portion control:** Use the plate method – half non‑starchy veggies, quarter lean protein, quarter complex carbs.
- **Fluid management:** Follow any fluid restrictions; otherwise, sip water evenly.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Sodium restriction:** ≤ 2300 mg/day (≈ 1 tsp salt) or lower per physician advice.
- **Potassium monitoring:** Adjust intake based on labs; limit foods high in potassium when needed.
- **Blood pressure‑lowering nutrients:** Increase potassium‑rich (if allowed) and [magnesium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/magnesium/min-mg-1)‑rich foods (pumpkin seeds, leafy greens).

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Antihypertensive Medications (ACE‑I, ARBs, diuretics)** | Directly lowers blood pressure, slows kidney damage, proteinuria reduction. | Side effects (cough, electrolyte imbalance), pill burden.
| **Nutritional Management (DASH‑style diet)** | Improves blood pressure, reduces sodium load, supports overall cardiovascular health. | Requires strict adherence, may need monitoring of potassium/phosphorus.
| **Combined Approach** | Best outcomes for BP control and renal preservation. | Requires coordination between doctor and dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

<details><summary>1. Hypertensive nephrosclerosis ka main cause kya hai?</summary>Long‑term uncontrolled high blood pressure damages kidney blood vessels.
</details>
<details><summary>2. Sodium intake kitna kam karna chahiye?</summary>Typically ≤ 2300 mg per day; many patients aim for 1500 mg.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Potassium restriction kab shuru karen?</summary>When eGFR falls below 45 mL/min/1.73 m² or labs show hyperkalemia.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Protein intake kitna hona chahiye?</summary>≈ 0.8 g/kg body weight per day, adjusted for CKD stage.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Kya processed meats avoid karne chahiye?</summary>Yes, they are high in sodium, saturated fat, and phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>6. Blood pressure ko diet se kaise improve karen?</summary>Follow DASH diet – high fiber, low sodium, moderate potassium, healthy fats.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Kya alcohol allowed hai?</summary>Limited intake (≤ 1 drink/day) and monitor blood pressure.
</details>
<details><summary>8. Fluid restriction ki zaroorat kab hoti hai?</summary>Usually when edema or hypertension is uncontrolled, as per physician.
</details>
<details><summary>9. Kya low‑fat dairy safe hain?</summary>Low‑phosphorus alternatives like almond milk are preferred; regular dairy may be high in phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>10. Exercise ka role kya hai?</summary>Regular aerobic exercise (30 min most days) helps lower blood pressure.
</details>
<details><summary>11. Kya salt substitutes safe hain?</summary>Often contain potassium chloride – not recommended if potassium restriction needed.
</details>
<details><summary>12. How often should labs be checked?</summary>Every 3–6 months for BP, eGFR, electrolytes.
</details>
<details><summary>13. What are common symptoms?</summary>Often asymptomatic; may have hypertension, mild edema, or reduced urine output.
</details>
<details><summary>14. Can I use herbal supplements?</summary>Generally not without physician approval; some may affect BP or kidney function.
</details>
<details><summary>15. When to see a renal dietitian?</summary>At diagnosis, when labs show electrolyte issues, or when dietary changes become complex.
</details>
