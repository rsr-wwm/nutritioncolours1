## Clinical Definition & Overview

**Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)** ek long‑term condition hai jahan kidneys ki filtering ability dhire‑dhire kam hoti jati hai, usually measured by eGFR. CKD stages 1‑5 define severity, with stage 5 (kidney failure) often requiring dialysis or transplant. Nutrition plays a central role in slowing disease progression, managing complications (e.g., electrolyte imbalance, anemia), and maintaining overall health.

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **High‑Quality, Moderate‑Protein Sources:** Egg whites, skinless chicken, fish, paneer, and plant‑based proteins (dal, beans) – aim for 0.8 g/kg body weight per day (adjust per stage).
- **Low‑[Potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1) Fruits & Vegetables:** [Apples](/knowledge/fruits/pome-stone-fruits/drupes/apple), berries, cabbage, [cauliflower](/knowledge/vegetables/cruciferous-vegetables/flowering-brassicas/brassica-oleracea-var-botrytis), bell peppers.
- **Complex Carbohydrates:** White rice, oats, whole‑grain breads (if potassium is controlled). Adjust portion size based on blood sugar.
- **Healthy Fats:** Olive oil, canola oil, nuts/seeds (limited quantity for calorie needs).

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑[Sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) Foods:** Processed snacks, canned soups, fast food, pickles.
- **High‑Potassium Foods (especially in stages 4‑5):** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, avocados.
- **High‑[Phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1) Foods:** Dairy products, nuts, seeds, cola beverages, processed meats.
- **Excessive Protein:** Large portions of red meat or protein supplements beyond prescribed limits.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Small Frequent Meals:** 4‑5 meals daily to maintain steady blood glucose and reduce uremic symptoms.
- **Portion Control:** Use the plate method – half veggies, quarter protein, quarter complex carbs.
- **Fluid Management:** Follow physician’s fluid restriction; sip water throughout the day, use ice chips if thirsty.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Diabetic CKD:** Prioritize low‑glycemic index carbs, monitor blood sugar.
- **Hypertensive CKD:** Follow DASH principles – low sodium, high fiber.
- **Stage‑Specific Adjustments:** Reduce potassium & phosphorus as eGFR declines; increase protein in dialysis‑dependent stages.

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pharmacological Therapy (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, phosphate binders, etc.)** | Controls blood pressure, reduces proteinuria, corrects electrolyte imbalances quickly. | Side effects, pill burden, cost; does not address overall diet quality.
| **Nutritional Management (Dietary Adjustments)** | Slows disease progression, improves quality of life, reduces medication dosage needs. | Requires strict adherence, frequent monitoring, risk of malnutrition if not balanced.
| **Combined Approach** | Offers best clinical outcomes by targeting both metabolic control and lifestyle. | Increased care complexity; requires coordination between nephrologist and dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

<details><summary>1. CKD ka sabse common cause kya hai?</summary>Diabetes aur high blood pressure (hypertension) sabse common risk factors hain.
</details>
<details><summary>2. CKD me protein intake kitna hona chahiye?</summary>Generally 0.8 g/kg body weight per day, but may be individualized based on stage and dialysis status.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Potassium restriction ki importance kya hai?</summary>Failing kidneys excess potassium excrete nahi kar pati, jo cardiac arrhythmia ka risk badhata hai.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Phosphorus ko limit karna kyun zaroori hai?</summary>High phosphorus leads to vascular calcification and bone disease.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Sodium restriction kaise blood pressure ko affect karta hai?</summary>Low sodium reduces fluid retention, helping control hypertension.
</details>
<details><summary>6. CKD patients ko kaunse fluids avoid karne chahiye?</summary>High‑sugar drinks, sodas, and large amounts of fruit juices; stick to water, herbal teas, and low‑potassium options.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Dialysis ke baad diet me kya changes aate hain?</summary>Protein needs increase (1.2‑1.3 g/kg), potassium & phosphorus may be less restricted depending on modality.
</details>
<details><summary>8. CKD me weight loss kaise prevent karen?</summary>Ensure adequate calories from carbs & fats, monitor serum albumin, consider renal‑specific supplements.
</details>
<details><summary>9. Kya CKD patients ko vegetarian diet follow karni chahiye?</summary>Possible, but must carefully manage protein quality, potassium, and phosphorus.
</details>
<details><summary>10. [Calcium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/calcium/min-ca-1) supplements safe hain?</summary>Only renal‑appropriate calcium formulations; excess can worsen vascular calcification.
</details>
<details><summary>11. Kya herbal teas safe hain?</summary>Most are safe in moderation, but avoid those with high potassium or nephrotoxic herbs.
</details>
<details><summary>12. CKD me anemia kaise manage karen?</summary>Iron‑rich foods, renal‑specific erythropoietin therapy, and adequate vitamin B12/folate.
</details>
<details><summary>13. Kya low‑sodium salts safe hain?</summary>Often potassium‑based and can increase potassium load – not recommended for CKD.
</details>
<details><summary>14. How often should I get blood tests?</summary>Every 1‑3 months to monitor eGFR, electrolytes, and anemia.
</details>
<details><summary>15. When should I see a dietitian?</summary>At diagnosis, whenever stage changes, or when symptoms like malnutrition arise.
</details>
