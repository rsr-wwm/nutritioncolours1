# Renal Imaging & Biopsy Nutrition

## Clinical Definition
Renal imaging (ultrasound, CT, MRI) and percutaneous kidney biopsy are essential diagnostics for evaluating structural kidney disease, tumors, and nephropathies. Imaging may involve iodinated or gadolinium contrast, while biopsy requires aseptic technique and post‑procedure monitoring.

## Nutrition
### What to Eat (Pre‑Procedure)
- **Light meals** – clear broth, toast, applesauce 2‑4 hours before contrast studies.
- **Hydrating fluids** – 500 ml of water or clear electrolyte solution unless NPO (nil per os) is ordered.
- **Low‑fat protein** – boiled egg white, low‑fat yogurt if allowed, to maintain energy.
- **Avoid heavy, fatty foods** – fried items, dairy‑rich meals that may delay gastric emptying.

### What to Avoid (Pre‑Procedure)
- **High‑fat meals** – may delay contrast absorption and increase nausea.
- **Alcohol** – at least 24 hours before to reduce dehydration risk.
- **Caffeinated beverages** – may cause diuresis and affect contrast timing.
- **Large protein loads** – can raise serum creatinine transiently.

### Post‑Procedure Nutrition
- **Gradual re‑introduction** – start with clear liquids (water, broth) for 2‑4 hours, then light solid foods.
- **Protein for healing** – lean poultry, fish, tofu, or low‑fat dairy to support tissue repair.
- **Iron‑rich foods** – spinach, lentils, fortified cereals if anemia risk from biopsy.
- **Anti‑inflammatory foods** – turmeric‑spiced dishes, omega‑3 rich fish to aid recovery.

### Timing & Meal Planning
- **NPO compliance**: Follow exact fasting instructions (typically NPO after midnight for morning procedures).
- **Hydration**: Resume normal fluid intake after the procedure unless physician restricts due to contrast load.
- **Small, frequent meals**: Post‑biopsy, eat every 3‑4 hours to maintain energy and support wound healing.

### Special Considerations
- **Contrast allergies**: Patients with iodine allergy should avoid foods high in iodine (seaweed, iodized salt) before contrast studies.
- **Kidney function**: If eGFR < 60, ensure adequate hydration to protect renal function after contrast.
- **Bleeding risk**: Maintain vitamin K‑rich foods (leafy greens) if anticoagulants are held.

## Frequently Asked Questions
<details><summary>Do I need to fast before a kidney biopsy?</summary>
Usually NPO after midnight; follow your clinician’s specific instructions.
</details>

<details><summary>Can I drink water before a contrast CT?</summary>
Often allowed up to 2 hours before; verify with the imaging center.
</details>

<details><summary>What foods should I avoid if I’m allergic to iodine?</summary>
Limit seaweed, kelp, iodized salt, and some dairy products.
</details>

<details><summary>How long after contrast can I resume normal diet?</summary>
Typically within a few hours once you’re fully awake and no nausea persists.
</details>

<details><summary>Is a high‑protein meal okay after biopsy?</summary>
Yes, moderate protein aids tissue repair; avoid excessive amounts initially.
</details>

<details><summary>Should I take any supplements before the procedure?</summary>
Only if prescribed; avoid vitamin C high doses as they may affect contrast clearance.
</details>

<details><summary>What is the role of iron after a biopsy?</summary>
Iron supports red blood cell production if there’s blood loss; include iron‑rich foods.
</details>

<details><summary>Can I have coffee on the day of imaging?</summary>
Avoid caffeine on the day of contrast studies to reduce diuresis.
</details>

<details><summary>Do I need to continue medications?</summary>
Follow guidance on anticoagulants and antihypertensives; many are held briefly.
</details>

<details><summary>How much fluid should I drink after contrast?</summary>
At least 1–2 L of water over the next 24 hours to aid clearance.
</details>

<details><summary>Is it safe to eat spicy foods post‑procedure?</summary>
Mild spices are fine; avoid heavy, irritating foods if you have soreness.
</details>

<details><summary>What about sugary drinks?</summary>
Limit sugary drinks; they can increase osmotic load on kidneys.
</details>

<details><summary>Can I exercise after a biopsy?</summary>
Light activity after 24 hours; avoid heavy lifting for 48 hours.
</details>

<details><summary>Do I need special foods if I have diabetes?</summary>
Maintain blood glucose control; choose low‑glycemic carbs around the procedure.
</details>

<details><summary>What are the signs of complications to report immediately?</summary>
Severe flank pain, fever, continuous hematuria (visible blood in urine), or difficulty urinating should be reported to your doctor immediately.
</details>
