## Clinical Definition & Overview

**Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)** ek sudden loss of kidney function hota hai, jisme kidneys blood ko filter karne ki ability temporarily lose kar deti hain. This can be caused by dehydration, sepsis, certain medications, or direct kidney injury. The goal is early detection, fluid management, and supportive nutrition to prevent further damage and aid recovery.

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **High-Quality Proteins (moderate):** Egg whites, lean chicken, fish, tofu – limited to prevent urea buildup while supporting tissue repair.
- **Low‑[Potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1) Fruits & Vegetables:** [Apples](/knowledge/fruits/pome-stone-fruits/drupes/apple), berries, cabbage, [cauliflower](/knowledge/vegetables/cruciferous-vegetables/flowering-brassicas/brassica-oleracea-var-botrytis).
- **Complex Carbohydrates:** White rice, white bread, pasta – easier on kidneys and provide energy.
- **Healthy Fats:** Olive oil, canola oil – provide calories without excess protein.

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑Potassium Foods:** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes.
- **High‑[Phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1) Foods:** Dairy, nuts, processed meats.
- **High‑[Sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) Foods:** Processed snacks, canned soups, fast food.
- **Excessive Protein:** Red meat, large portions of meat.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Small Frequent Meals:** 4–6 small meals per day to maintain energy and reduce uremic symptoms.
- **Portion Control:** Use measuring cups for protein and high‑potassium foods.
- **Fluid Management:** If fluid restriction is advised, spread intake throughout the day; use ice chips for thirst.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Renal Vitamins:** Only take renal‑specific multivitamins prescribed by a nephrologist.
- **Avoid NSAIDs:** Non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs can worsen kidney injury.

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medical Therapy (Dialysis, Diuretics, Electrolyte Management)** | Rapid correction of fluid/electrolyte imbalances; life‑saving in severe AKI. | Invasive, risk of infection, may require hospitalization.
| **Nutritional Management (Dietary Adjustments)** | Supports kidney recovery, reduces toxin buildup, improves patient comfort. | Requires strict adherence, may be restrictive, risk of malnutrition if not monitored.
| **Combined Approach** | Optimizes recovery while mitigating side effects of each alone. | Higher care burden; needs coordinated monitoring.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

<details><summary>1. AKI ka main cause kya hota hai?</summary>Common causes include dehydration, severe infections (sepsis), certain drugs (e.g., aminoglycosides), and reduced blood flow to kidneys.
</details>
<details><summary>2. AKI me protein intake kaise manage karen?</summary>Typically 0.8 g/kg body weight per day, adjusted individually; focus on high‑quality proteins.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Potassium restriction kyun zaroori hai?</summary>Damaged kidneys cannot excrete excess potassium, leading to hyperkalemia which can cause dangerous heart rhythms.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Fluid intake ko kaise limit karen?</summary>Follow physician's fluid prescription; sip water slowly, use ice chips, and avoid high‑water foods.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Phosphorus ko limit karne ke kya options hain?</summary>Choose low‑phosphorus dairy alternatives (e.g., almond milk), avoid processed meats and sodas, read labels for phosphate additives.
</details>
<details><summary>6. AKI me diet se fatigue kaise kam ho sakta hai?</summary>Ensuring adequate calories from carbs and fats, and balanced electrolytes helps maintain energy levels.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Kya fasting safe hai?</summary>Generally not recommended during AKI as it can worsen dehydration and electrolyte imbalance.
</details>
<details><summary>8. Sodium restriction se blood pressure par kya effect hota hai?</summary>Reduces fluid retention, helping control blood pressure and swelling.
</details>
<details><summary>9. AKI ke baad diet ko kaise transition karen?</summary>Gradually re‑introduce higher‑protein foods as kidney function improves, under dietitian guidance.
</details>
<details><summary>10. Dialysis ki zaroorat se pehle diet kya karna chahiye?</summary>Focus on low‑potassium, low‑phosphorus, moderate protein, and fluid control to delay need for dialysis.
</details>
<details><summary>11. Kya herbal supplements safe hain?</summary>Most are not recommended without physician approval due to unknown kidney effects.
</details>
<details><summary>12. AKI me weight loss kaise monitor karen?</summary>Track daily weight; rapid gains may indicate fluid overload, losses may indicate malnutrition.
</details>
<details><summary>13. Kya coffee allowed hai?</summary>Limit to 1‑2 cups if fluid-restricted; avoid added sugar and high‑fat cream.
</details>
<details><summary>14. Kab doctor ko immediately inform karen?</summary>If you experience shortness of breath, severe swelling, chest pain, or sudden change in urine output.
</details>
<details><summary>15. AKI ke baad diet plan ka duration kya hai?</summary>Depends on recovery; often 2‑4 weeks of modified diet, then gradual normalization.
</details>
