## Clinical Definition & Overview

**Glomerular diseases** refer to a group of conditions that affect the glomeruli – the tiny filtering units in the kidneys. Common examples include IgA nephropathy, membranous nephropathy, and lupus nephritis. These conditions often present with proteinuria, hematuria, and reduced kidney function. While specific treatments vary, dietary management aims to reduce inflammation, control blood pressure, and limit renal workload.

## Nutrition Section

### Foods to Eat (Kya Khayein)
- **Anti‑Inflammatory Proteins:** Skinless poultry, fish (especially fatty fish like salmon for omega‑3), egg whites, tofu – 0.8‑1.0 g/kg body weight, adjusted per stage.
- **Low‑[Potassium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/potassium/min-k-1) Vegetables & Fruits:** Berries, apples, cabbage, cauliflower, green beans, bell peppers.
- **Omega‑3 Rich Sources:** Salmon, sardines, chia seeds (small portions due to phosphorus) – help reduce glomerular inflammation.
- **Complex Carbohydrates:** White rice, white bread, pasta – easy on kidneys and provide energy.
- **Healthy Fats:** Olive oil, canola oil – calorie dense without excess protein.

### Foods to Avoid (Kya Na Khayein)
- **High‑[Sodium](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/sodium-chloride/min-na-1) Processed Foods:** Canned soups, snack foods, fast food.
- **High‑Potassium Produce (if eGFR < 45):** Bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes.
- **High‑[Phosphorus](/knowledge/nutrient-database/mineral-reference-data/phosphorus/min-p-1) Items:** Dairy, nuts, seeds, cola drinks, processed meats.
- **Excessive Red Meat & Protein Supplements:** Can increase glomerular pressure and uremic toxins.

### Meal Timing & Portion Control
- **Frequent Small Meals:** 4‑5 meals daily to avoid large protein loads.
- **Portion Control:** Use measuring cups for protein and potassium‑rich foods.
- **Fluid Management:** Follow physician’s fluid restriction; sip water throughout the day.

### Special Dietary Considerations
- **Blood Pressure Control:** Follow DASH principles – low sodium, high fiber.
- **Proteinuria Management:** Moderate protein intake; consider low‑protein diet if proteinuria is severe and eGFR is low.
- **Stage‑Specific Adjustments:** Reduce potassium & phosphorus as kidney function declines.

## Pros & Cons Analysis: Medical Therapy vs. Nutritional Management

| Approach | Pros (Fayde) | Cons (Nuksan) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immunosuppressive Therapy (corticosteroids, cyclophosphamide, rituximab)** | Directly reduces immune‑mediated glomerular inflammation; can halt disease progression. | Side effects (infection risk, weight gain, hypertension), pill burden.
| **Nutritional Management** | Lowers inflammatory load, supports blood pressure control, reduces renal workload. | Requires strict adherence; risk of malnutrition if protein is too restricted.
| **Combined Approach** | Optimizes disease control while preserving nutrition. | Coordination needed among nephrologist, rheumatologist, dietitian.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

</details>
<details><summary>2. Proteinuria ko diet se kaise manage karen?</summary>Moderate protein intake (0.8 g/kg) and consider low‑protein diet if protein loss is high.
</details>
<details><summary>3. Omega‑3 fatty acids ka benefit kya hai?</summary>Anti‑inflammatory effects that may reduce glomerular injury.
</details>
<details><summary>4. Sodium restriction kyun zaruri hai?</summary>Helps control blood pressure and reduces fluid overload.
</details>
<details><summary>5. Potassium ko limit kab karna chahiye?</summary>When eGFR drops below 45 mL/min/1.73 m² or if hyperkalemia is present.
</details>
<details><summary>6. Phosphorus restriction ka impact kya hota hai?</summary>Prevents vascular calcification and secondary hyperparathyroidism.
</details>
<details><summary>7. Kya dairy completely avoid karna chahiye?</summary>Limit high‑phosphorus dairy; low‑phosphorus alternatives (almond milk) are preferred.
</details>
<details><summary>8. How often should labs be checked?</summary>Every 1‑3 months for eGFR, proteinuria, electrolytes.
</details>
<details><summary>9. Kya fasting safe hai?</summary>Generally not recommended; can cause dehydration & electrolyte shifts.
</details>
<details><summary>10. Kya exercise recommended hai?</summary>Yes, low‑impact aerobic activity improves cardiovascular health, but avoid heavy weightlifting if uncontrolled hypertension.
</details>
<details><summary>11. Kya herbal supplements safe hain?</summary>Most are not recommended without physician approval due to potential nephrotoxicity.
</details>
<details><summary>12. How does blood pressure affect glomerular disease?</summary>Hypertension raises glomerular pressure, accelerating damage.
</details>
<details><summary>13. Can I use salt substitutes?</summary>Usually contain potassium chloride; avoid if potassium needs restriction.\n</details>
<details><summary>14. What is the role of vitamin D?</summary>Vitamin D deficiency is common; supplementation should be renal‑specific.
</details>
<details><summary>15. When should I see a renal dietitian?</summary>At diagnosis, during flare‑ups, or when dietary changes become complex.
</details>
