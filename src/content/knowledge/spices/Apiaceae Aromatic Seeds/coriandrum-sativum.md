## Overview

*Scientific name: Coriandrum sativum*

Coriander seed (dhania) is the dried fruit of the cilantro plant, used whole or ground; per-100g reference values are far higher in energy and minerals than a typical spice serving (0.5–2 g) would actually provide.

> **Serving note:** Typical culinary serving is 0.5–2 g (a pinch to a teaspoon) — composition below is a per-100g reference scale, not a realistic single serving.

**Key nutrients:** Iron, Magnesium, Calcium, Fiber (by dry weight)

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** A dense source of iron, magnesium, and calcium by dry weight, though realistic culinary quantities contribute only a small fraction of daily requirements.
- **[Traditional / insufficient evidence]** Traditionally used to support digestion; early research explores coriander extracts for mild antimicrobial and antioxidant activity — clinical significance at culinary doses is not established. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Dry-roasting coriander seed briefly before grinding intensifies its citrusy aroma; ground coriander loses potency faster than whole seed.
