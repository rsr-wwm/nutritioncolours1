## Overview

*Scientific name: Foeniculum vulgare*

Fennel seed (saunf) is a sweet, aromatic seed spice traditionally chewed after meals in Indian households; per-100g reference values are far higher than a typical serving (0.5–2 g) would provide.

> **Serving note:** Typical culinary/traditional serving is 0.5–2 g — composition below is a per-100g reference scale.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g reference scale)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 345 kcal |
| Water | 8.81 g |
| Carbohydrate | 52.29 g |
| Fiber | 39.8 g |
| Protein | 15.8 g |
| Fat | 14.87 g |
| Calcium | 1196 mg |
| Iron | 18.54 mg |
| Potassium | 1694 mg |
| Manganese | 6.5 mg |

**Key nutrients:** Calcium, Iron, Potassium, Manganese

## What the evidence says

- **[Limited evidence]** Traditionally used as a post-meal digestive aid in Indian households; some clinical research on fennel extract supports mild carminative (gas-relieving) effects, though at doses higher than typical chewing quantities. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Lightly toasting fennel seed before use brings out its anise-like aroma; it is traditionally chewed plain or with a little sugar as a post-meal digestive and breath freshener.
