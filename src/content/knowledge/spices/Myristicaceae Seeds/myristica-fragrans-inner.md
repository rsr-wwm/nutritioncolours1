## Overview

*Scientific name: Myristica fragrans*

Nutmeg (jaiphal) is the ground inner seed of the Myristica fragrans fruit, used in small pinches in both sweet and savory dishes; it contains myristicin, a compound with dose-dependent psychoactive and toxic effects at high (non-culinary) doses.

> **Serving note:** Typical culinary serving is well under 1 g (a pinch to 1/4 teaspoon) — composition below is a per-100g reference scale, and is not a safety guide for concentrated nutmeg doses.

**Key nutrients:** Manganese, Copper, Iron, Folate

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** At normal culinary pinches, nutmeg poses no known safety concern; at gram-scale doses (well beyond cooking use), myristicin can cause toxic, hallucinogenic effects — a dose-response relationship, not a reason to avoid culinary use.

## Preparation intelligence

Grating whole nutmeg fresh just before use preserves its volatile aromatic oils far better than pre-ground nutmeg.
