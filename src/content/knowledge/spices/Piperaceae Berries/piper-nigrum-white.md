## Overview

*Scientific name: Piper nigrum*

White pepper is the same fruit as black pepper (Piper nigrum), processed differently — the outer skin is removed after retting — giving a milder, less pungent flavor without visible dark flecks; per-100g reference values are far higher than a typical serving (0.5–2 g) would provide.

> **Serving note:** Typical culinary serving is 0.5–2 g — composition below is a per-100g reference scale.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g reference scale)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 296 kcal |
| Fiber | 26.2 g |
| Protein | 10.4 g |
| Manganese | 4 mg |

**Key nutrients:** Manganese, Fiber (by dry weight)

## What the evidence says

- **[Limited evidence]** Nutritionally similar to black pepper (same source plant), with retting/processing altering flavor compounds more than core mineral composition. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

White pepper is preferred in light-colored dishes (white sauces, some Chinese and Southeast Asian preparations) where black pepper's dark flecks are visually undesirable; its retting process changes but does not eliminate piperine content.
