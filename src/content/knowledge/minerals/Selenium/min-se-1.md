## Overview

Selenium is built into antioxidant selenoproteins such as glutathione peroxidase and is essential for thyroid hormone metabolism, reproduction, and DNA synthesis.

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Men 19+ | 55 mcg |
| Women 19+ | 55 mcg |
| Pregnancy | 60 mcg |
| Lactation | 70 mcg |

**Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 400 mcg/day for adults. Above this, selenosis can develop.

## Food sources

Brazil nuts are extraordinarily rich (roughly 68–91 mcg per nut) — just 1–3 a day usually meets needs, and eating many daily can exceed the safe limit. Seafood, meat, poultry, eggs, and grains also contribute; content in plant foods depends heavily on soil selenium.

## Deficiency

Deficiency is uncommon where soil is selenium-rich but can cause Keshan disease (a heart disorder) and impaired thyroid and immune function.

## Too much

Selenosis (chronic excess) causes hair loss, brittle nails, a garlic breath odour, a metallic taste, and neurological problems.
