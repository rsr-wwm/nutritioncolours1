## Overview

Iodine is an essential building block of the thyroid hormones thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), which regulate metabolism and are critical for brain development in pregnancy and infancy.

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Men 19+ | 150 mcg |
| Women 19+ | 150 mcg |
| Pregnancy | 220 mcg |
| Lactation | 290 mcg |

**Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 1,100 mcg (1.1 mg)/day for adults. Both too little and too much iodine can cause thyroid dysfunction.

## Food sources

Iodized salt, seafood (cod, tuna, shrimp), seaweed (which can be extremely concentrated), dairy products, and eggs. People using non-iodized specialty salts or plant-based milks may get less.

## Deficiency

Deficiency causes goiter (enlarged thyroid) and hypothyroidism; during pregnancy it is a leading preventable cause of intellectual disability in children.

## Too much

Excess (often from seaweed or supplements) can paradoxically cause both hypo- and hyperthyroidism, especially in susceptible people.
