## Overview

Chromium (as trivalent chromium, the dietary form) enhances the action of insulin and so plays a role in carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism. Note: hexavalent chromium is an industrial toxin and is unrelated to the dietary nutrient.

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Men 19–50 | 35 mcg (AI) |
| Men 51+ | 30 mcg (AI) |
| Women 19–50 | 25 mcg (AI) |
| Women 51+ | 20 mcg (AI) |

**Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** No Tolerable Upper Intake Level has been established, because data on adverse effects from food chromium were insufficient. (Values are Adequate Intakes, AI.)

## Food sources

Meat, whole grains, broccoli, potatoes, legumes, and nuts. Amounts in food vary widely and depend partly on processing (stainless-steel equipment can add small amounts).

## Deficiency

Overt deficiency is rare and mainly documented in older studies of prolonged intravenous feeding.

## Too much

Dietary chromium is considered safe; supplement claims for weight loss or blood-sugar control are not strongly supported by evidence.
