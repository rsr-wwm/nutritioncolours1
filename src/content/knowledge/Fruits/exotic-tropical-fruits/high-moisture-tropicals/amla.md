## Overview

*Scientific name: Phyllanthus emblica*

Amla is an Indian fruit exceptionally high in vitamin C and polyphenols, used traditionally in Ayurveda and studied for lipid and glycemic effects.


## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 44 kcal |
| Water | 87.9 g |
| Carbohydrate | 10.2 g |
| Fiber | 4.3 g |
| Protein | 0.88 g |
| Fat | 0.58 g |
| Vitamin C | 27.7 mg |
| Calcium | 25 mg |

**Glycemic note:** Very low sugar; usually eaten in small amounts.

**Key nutrients:** Vitamin C, Polyphenols (emblicanin), Fiber

**Seasonality (India):** Winter

## What the evidence says

- **[Traditional / insufficient evidence]** Traditional Ayurvedic use for digestion and rejuvenation (Rasayana) — framed as traditional use, not clinical proof.
- **[Limited evidence]** Small trials suggest amla extract may improve lipid profiles; evidence is preliminary. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Vitamin C in amla is unusually heat-stable because tannins protect it during cooking/drying.
