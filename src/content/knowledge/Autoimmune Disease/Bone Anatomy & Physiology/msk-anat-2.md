## Overview

Synovial joints (like the knee, hip, and shoulder) allow free movement and are cushioned by articular cartilage, lubricated by synovial fluid, and stabilized by ligaments (bone-to-bone) and tendons (muscle-to-bone). This is anatomical background rather than a disease topic, so this overview is intentionally brief.

## Nutritional Relevance

Cartilage and connective tissue are largely made of collagen and proteoglycans, which depend on adequate dietary protein and vitamin C (a required cofactor for collagen synthesis) to maintain and repair. Beyond that, general joint health is supported more by maintaining a healthy body weight — which reduces mechanical load on cartilage — and staying physically active than by any specific "joint supplement." Popular supplements like glucosamine and chondroitin have mixed clinical trial evidence and should be discussed with a physician rather than assumed effective.

## Safety Note

This is educational information, not medical advice. Joint pain, swelling, or instability should be evaluated by a physician or orthopedist rather than managed through diet alone.
