## Overview

This group of vasculitides affects medium and large blood vessels and includes giant cell arteritis (GCA, or temporal arteritis), Takayasu arteritis ("pulseless disease"), polyarteritis nodosa (PAN), and related conditions such as Kawasaki disease, IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein purpura), cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, and Behçet's disease. GCA in particular often requires prolonged corticosteroid treatment to prevent vision loss and other complications, which makes corticosteroid-related nutrition support especially relevant here.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Long-term corticosteroid support:** Because GCA frequently requires steroid tapers lasting a year or more, sustained attention to calcium and vitamin D intake for bone protection, sodium moderation for blood pressure, and blood glucose monitoring is particularly important.
- **Cardiovascular health:** Large vessel vasculitis affects the arterial wall directly, so a heart-healthy dietary pattern (vegetables, fruit, whole grains, healthy fats, limited saturated fat and sodium) supports overall vascular health alongside medical treatment.
- **Weight-bearing exercise and nutrition:** Combined with adequate protein, calcium, and vitamin D, weight-bearing activity (as tolerated and approved by the care team) helps offset steroid-related bone loss.
- **Behçet's disease and oral/GI ulcers:** For those with Behçet's-related mouth or GI ulcers, softer, less acidic, and less spicy foods may improve comfort during flares.
- **General principles:** As with other vasculitides, no specific diet has been shown to treat or reverse large vessel vasculitis; nutrition here supports overall health and manages treatment side effects.

## Safety Note

Giant cell arteritis is a medical urgency when new visual symptoms occur, and all forms of large vessel vasculitis require specialist diagnosis and monitoring. Nutrition strategies described here support — but do not replace — corticosteroid or immunosuppressive therapy directed by a rheumatologist.
