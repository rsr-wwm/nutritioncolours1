## Overview

Treatment for systemic sclerosis depends on which organs are affected. Immunosuppressants (methotrexate, mycophenolate, cyclophosphamide, tocilizumab) target skin and lung fibrosis; antifibrotics such as nintedanib address interstitial lung disease; vasodilators (bosentan, sildenafil) and prostacyclin analogs like iloprost manage pulmonary arterial hypertension and digital ulcers; and in select severe cases, autologous stem cell transplantation is considered. Nutrition needs during these treatments center on tolerating GI symptoms and supporting the body through immunosuppression, rather than any single "treatment diet."

## Nutritional Considerations

- **GI tolerance:** The reflux and dysmotility strategies used broadly in systemic sclerosis (small frequent meals, staying upright after eating, limiting reflux triggers) remain relevant during treatment.
- **Immunosuppression and food safety:** Immunosuppressive agents raise infection risk, so thorough food safety practices — proper cooking temperatures, avoiding unpasteurized products, careful produce washing — are important.
- **Digital ulcers and circulation:** While diet does not directly heal digital ulcers, adequate overall nutrition and avoiding tobacco (which worsens vasospasm) support tissue healing alongside vasodilator therapy.
- **Around stem cell transplant:** Patients undergoing autologous stem cell transplantation typically receive individualized nutrition protocols from their transplant center, including specific food-safety precautions during the neutropenic period — these instructions supersede general dietary guidance.
- **Bone and cardiovascular health:** Ongoing monitoring of bone density and cardiovascular risk factors remains relevant, with calcium, vitamin D, and a heart-healthy eating pattern as reasonable general supports.

## Safety Note

All of these therapies require specialist oversight, and stem cell transplant in particular involves a highly individualized peri-transplant nutrition protocol set by the treating center. Do not adjust immunosuppressant, vasodilator, or antifibrotic therapy based on diet, and report any signs of infection or worsening digital ulcers promptly.
