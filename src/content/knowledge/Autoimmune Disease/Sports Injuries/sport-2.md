## Overview

Rotator cuff tears (partial or full-thickness), shoulder dislocations, and subacromial impingement are shoulder conditions diagnosed by physical exam and imaging, and treated with physical therapy or, for more severe injuries, arthroscopic repair or shoulder replacement. This is orthopedic and sports-medicine territory, not a nutrition topic, so this page stays brief.

## Nutritional Considerations (Recovery Support)

As with other soft-tissue injuries, nutrition's real contribution is during recovery: adequate protein intake supports muscle and tendon repair, vitamin C supports collagen synthesis, and an anti-inflammatory eating pattern is a reasonable general approach alongside physical therapy. These support healing but do not substitute for appropriate orthopedic treatment.

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Shoulder pain, instability, or suspected dislocation should be evaluated by an orthopedic specialist; treatment and rehabilitation decisions should be made with that specialist and a physical therapist.
