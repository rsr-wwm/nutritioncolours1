## Overview

Total knee, total hip, and revision joint replacement are surgical procedures performed by orthopedic surgeons for advanced joint disease that no longer responds to conservative management. The decision to have surgery, the surgical approach, and post-operative rehabilitation protocols are entirely within the scope of orthopedic surgery and physical therapy — not nutrition — and are not covered in clinical detail here.

---

## Nutritional Considerations (Peri-Operative Support)

What nutrition can legitimately support is recovery around the time of surgery. Adequate protein intake in the weeks before and after surgery supports wound healing and helps preserve muscle mass during the temporary reduction in activity that follows joint replacement. Vitamin C is a required cofactor for collagen synthesis, which is central to wound repair, and zinc supports immune function and tissue repair as well. Maintaining stable blood sugar and a healthy weight before elective surgery is also associated with fewer post-surgical complications in the general surgical literature.

---

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Any questions about candidacy for joint replacement, surgical timing, or rehabilitation belong with an orthopedic surgeon. Discuss any planned changes to protein, vitamin, or mineral intake around surgery with your surgical team, particularly if you take blood thinners or have kidney disease.
