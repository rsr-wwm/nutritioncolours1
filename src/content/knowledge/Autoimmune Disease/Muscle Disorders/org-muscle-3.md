## Overview

This topic spans several distinct and serious neuromuscular conditions: muscular dystrophies (genetic, progressive muscle-wasting diseases such as Duchenne and Becker), myasthenia gravis (an autoimmune disorder affecting the neuromuscular junction), rhabdomyolysis (acute breakdown of muscle tissue that can injure the kidneys), muscle cramps, and metabolic myopathies (genetic disorders of muscle energy metabolism, such as McArdle disease). These are neurology- and, in rhabdomyolysis, emergency-medicine conditions requiring specialist diagnosis and treatment — not conditions that diet resolves.

---

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Muscular dystrophy and myasthenia gravis**: Adequate protein and calorie intake help counter disease-related muscle wasting, and calcium/vitamin D are relevant if corticosteroids (used in some muscular dystrophy treatment) are part of the regimen. For myasthenia gravis, if swallowing muscles are affected, texture-modified foods guided by a speech-language pathologist may be needed.
- **Rhabdomyolysis**: This is a medical emergency; aggressive IV fluid administration in a hospital setting, not dietary intervention, is the primary treatment. Adequate hydration in daily life is a reasonable general precaution for people at higher risk (e.g., during intense exercise in heat), but it does not substitute for emergency care once rhabdomyolysis is suspected.
- **Muscle cramps**: Cramps are sometimes linked to dehydration or low magnesium, calcium, potassium, or sodium; addressing hydration and electrolyte intake is a reasonable first step for benign, exercise-related cramps, though persistent cramps warrant medical evaluation for other causes.
- **Metabolic myopathies**: These genetic conditions (like McArdle disease) sometimes involve specific dietary strategies developed with a metabolic specialist or dietitian — this is highly individualized and should not be self-managed.

---

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Muscular dystrophy, myasthenia gravis, and metabolic myopathies require diagnosis and ongoing care from a neurologist. Dark urine after intense exertion, severe muscle pain and swelling, or any suspected rhabdomyolysis requires emergency medical care immediately.
