## Overview

Scoliosis (sideways spinal curvature) and kyphosis (excess forward curvature) are structural spinal deformities, most often diagnosed in adolescence (adolescent idiopathic scoliosis) or, less commonly, developing in adulthood from degenerative changes. Severity is tracked using the Cobb angle on X-ray, and management ranges from observation and bracing to spinal fusion surgery, all under orthopedic care. These are structural/developmental conditions, not conditions driven by diet, so this page stays brief.

## Nutritional Considerations

There is no dietary intervention proven to prevent, slow, or correct scoliosis or kyphosis curvature. General bone-supportive nutrition — adequate calcium, vitamin D, and protein during the adolescent growth years — supports overall skeletal health but does not treat the deformity itself. For adults undergoing spinal fusion surgery, standard peri-operative nutrition (protein, vitamin C, zinc) supports wound and bone-graft healing.

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Scoliosis screening, curve monitoring, and bracing or surgical decisions should be directed by an orthopedic spine specialist. Rapidly progressing curvature, new back pain, or neurological symptoms in a child or adult with scoliosis warrant prompt medical evaluation.
