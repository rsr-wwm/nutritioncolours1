## Overview

Ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, and axial spondyloarthritis are inflammatory forms of arthritis that primarily affect the spine and joints, often linked to the HLA-B27 gene. They are diagnosed and managed by rheumatologists using disease-modifying medications and biologics. While diet cannot reverse structural spinal changes, nutrition can support inflammation control, bone health, and weight management, all of which affect symptom burden.

---

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Anti-inflammatory dietary pattern**: A Mediterranean-style diet — vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, olive oil, and fatty fish — is the most evidence-supported nutritional approach for inflammatory arthritis generally, and is reasonable to recommend here, though it is not a cure.
- **Weight management**: Excess body weight increases mechanical load on the spine and lower-limb joints and is associated with poorer treatment response in spondyloarthritis; maintaining a healthy weight is a worthwhile goal.
- **Bone health**: Ankylosing spondylitis is associated with increased osteoporosis risk, partly from reduced mobility and chronic inflammation. Adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, per NIH Office of Dietary Supplements guidance, supports bone density.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids**: Fish oil has modest evidence for reducing inflammatory markers in some arthritis populations and may be a reasonable dietary inclusion (fatty fish, walnuts, flaxseed).
- **Elimination diets**: Popular claims about gluten-free or nightshade-free diets curing spondyloarthritis are not supported by strong clinical evidence; patients should be cautious about restrictive diets that aren't medically indicated, as they can lead to nutrient gaps.

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This content is educational, not medical advice. Ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, and axial spondyloarthritis require diagnosis and ongoing management by a rheumatologist. Report new eye pain/redness (uveitis is a known association), worsening spinal stiffness, or skin/nail changes to your care team promptly.
