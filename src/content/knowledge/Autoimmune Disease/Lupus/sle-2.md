## Overview

Lupus treatment is tailored to disease severity and organ involvement, ranging from hydroxychloroquine (a cornerstone therapy requiring periodic retinal screening) to corticosteroids, immunosuppressants such as mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, or cyclophosphamide, and biologics such as belimumab or anifrolumab. For women of childbearing age, pregnancy planning is an important part of lupus management, since certain medications must be adjusted before conception. Nutrition support during treatment is adjunctive and should be individualized to the specific regimen.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Hydroxychloroquine and GI tolerance:** Taking hydroxychloroquine with food is a commonly recommended practice to reduce stomach upset; specific dosing questions should go to the prescribing physician.
- **Corticosteroid-related needs:** When corticosteroids are part of the regimen, moderating sodium and monitoring blood glucose, along with adequate calcium and vitamin D for bone protection, are reasonable supportive steps.
- **Immunosuppressant food safety:** Medications such as mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine, or cyclophosphamide lower immune defenses; careful food handling and hygiene reduce infection risk during treatment.
- **Pregnancy planning:** Women planning pregnancy while managing lupus should ensure adequate folic acid intake and discuss medication safety with both their rheumatologist and obstetrician, since some lupus medications must be changed before or during pregnancy.
- **General balanced nutrition:** A varied, nutrient-adequate diet supports overall resilience during periods of immunosuppressive treatment, though it does not substitute for the medications themselves.

## Safety Note

Medication choices, dosing, and pregnancy planning in lupus must be directed by a rheumatologist (and obstetrician, where relevant). Do not start, stop, or substitute nutritional supplements for hydroxychloroquine, belimumab, or immunosuppressants without medical guidance.
