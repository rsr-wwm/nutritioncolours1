# Autoimmune Disease Nutrition

## Clinical Definition
Autoimmune diseases occur when the immune system mistakenly attacks the body’s own tissues, leading to chronic inflammation and organ damage. Examples include rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, multiple sclerosis, and inflammatory bowel disease. Management includes immunosuppressive therapy and lifestyle modifications, with nutrition playing a key role in modulating inflammation and supporting immune function.

## Nutrition
### What to Eat
- **Anti‑inflammatory foods** – fatty fish (EPA/DHA), walnuts, flaxseeds, chia seeds, olive oil, turmeric, ginger.
- **Gut‑friendly fibers** – soluble fiber from oats, psyllium, apples, carrots to support a healthy microbiome.
- **Vitamin‑rich produce** – berries, leafy greens, cruciferous vegetables provide antioxidants (vitamins C, E) and polyphenols.
- **Vitamin D‑rich foods** – fortified plant milks, mushrooms; essential for immune regulation.
- **Probiotic foods** – yogurt with live cultures, kefir, fermented vegetables (sauerkraut, kimchi) to promote beneficial gut bacteria.
- **Lean protein** – poultry, fish, legumes to maintain muscle mass without excess saturated fat.

### What to Avoid
- **Processed meats & high‑saturated‑fat foods** – increase systemic inflammation.
- **Added sugars & refined carbs** – promote inflammatory cytokines and dysbiosis.
- **Gluten (if sensitive)** – consider a gluten‑free trial for conditions like celiac disease.
- **Excessive alcohol** – can impair immune function and gut barrier.
- **Food additives & artificial sweeteners** – may trigger gut irritation.

### Timing & Meal Planning
- **Balanced meals**: Half plate vegetables, quarter lean protein, quarter whole grains.
- **Consistent schedule**: Regular meal timing helps stabilize blood glucose and cortisol rhythms.
- **Post‑exercise nutrition**: Protein + carbohydrate within 30 minutes to aid recovery.

### Special Considerations
- **Vitamin D status**: Check serum 25‑OH D; supplement to 1000‑2000 IU/day if deficient.
- **Omega‑3 dosage**: 1‑2 g EPA/DHA daily can reduce disease activity in rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Gut health**: Consider a low‑FODMAP trial for IBS‑type symptoms in autoimmune GI disorders.
- **Medication interactions**: Some supplements (e.g., high‑dose vitamin K) may interfere with anticoagulants used in lupus.

## Pros & Cons Table
| Aspect | Medical Management | Nutritional Approach |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Pros** | Immunosuppressants, biologics can control disease activity rapidly. | Diet can lower inflammation, improve gut microbiome, and reduce medication dosage.
| **Cons** | Medications may cause infections, organ toxicity, and high costs. | Strict dietary changes require discipline; risk of nutrient deficiencies if not monitored.

## Frequently Asked Questions
<details><summary>Can omega‑3 supplements help my arthritis?</summary>
Yes, 1–2 g EPA/DHA daily has been shown to modestly reduce joint pain and stiffness.
</details>

<details><summary>What foods support gut health?</summary>
Fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi) and prebiotic fibers (oats, bananas, asparagus).
</details>

<details><summary>Should I avoid gluten?</summary>
Only if you have celiac disease or a confirmed gluten sensitivity; otherwise, a balanced diet is fine.
</details>

<details><summary>Is a low‑sugar diet beneficial?</summary>
Reducing added sugars can lower inflammatory markers and improve blood glucose control.
</details>

<details><summary>How much vitamin D should I take?</summary>
Typically 1000–2000 IU/day, but adjust based on blood levels and physician advice.
</details>

<details><summary>Can I get enough calcium on a plant‑based diet?</summary>
Yes, fortified plant milks, tofu, leafy greens, and almonds provide calcium.
</details>

<details><summary>Do antioxidants help?</summary>
Antioxidant‑rich foods (berries, nuts, dark chocolate) may reduce oxidative stress.
</details>

<details><summary>What about coffee?</summary>
Moderate coffee (≤ 2 cups) is generally safe; avoid excess caffeine.
</details>

<details><summary>Is alcohol prohibited?</summary>
Limit to occasional moderate intake; heavy use can exacerbate inflammation.
</details>

<details><summary>Should I take a probiotic supplement?</summary>
Consider if you have digestive symptoms; choose a multi‑strain product with ≥ 10 billion CFU.
</details>

<details><summary>How often should I see a dietitian?</summary>
At diagnosis and when disease activity changes, then annually for monitoring.
</details>

<details><summary>Can weight loss improve symptoms?</summary>
Weight reduction can lower inflammatory load and improve joint function.
</details>

<details><summary>Is fasting beneficial?</summary>
Intermittent fasting may help some individuals, but discuss with your physician.
</details>

<details><summary>What spices have anti‑inflammatory properties?</summary>
Turmeric (curcumin), ginger, garlic, cinnamon.
</details>

<details><summary>How do I manage fatigue with diet?</summary>
Eat balanced meals, stay hydrated, and avoid large sugary spikes.
</details>
