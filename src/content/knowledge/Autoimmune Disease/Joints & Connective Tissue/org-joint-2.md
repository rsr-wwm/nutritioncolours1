## Overview

This topic spans several distinct joint conditions: rheumatoid arthritis (an autoimmune disease causing joint inflammation, treated with DMARDs and biologics), gout (covered in depth on our dedicated Gout pages), juvenile idiopathic arthritis, bursitis and tendinitis (inflammation of the fluid-filled sacs and tendons around joints), ligament injuries (like ACL and ankle sprains), and joint replacement surgery. Each has its own diagnostic pathway and is managed by rheumatology or orthopedics; this page gives a brief overview rather than duplicating the detailed protocols found elsewhere on this site.

---

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Rheumatoid arthritis**: An anti-inflammatory, Mediterranean-style eating pattern is a reasonable supportive approach alongside disease-modifying medication, which remains the primary treatment.
- **Gout**: See our dedicated Gout & Hyperuricemia page for detailed purine and uric acid nutrition guidance — this is one of the strongest diet-disease links in this category.
- **Bursitis, tendinitis, and ligament injuries**: General recovery nutrition (adequate protein, vitamin C for collagen repair, anti-inflammatory foods) supports healing alongside rest, physical therapy, and, when needed, injections or surgery.
- **Joint replacement**: Peri-operative protein, vitamin C, and zinc support wound healing; see our Joint Replacement Surgery page for more detail.

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Joint pain accompanied by swelling, warmth, fever, or inability to bear weight should be evaluated promptly by a physician to rule out infection or another serious cause. Diagnosis and treatment of any specific joint disorder should be directed by a rheumatologist or orthopedist.
