## Overview

Joints are classified as fibrous (immovable, like skull sutures), cartilaginous (slightly movable), or synovial (freely movable — the shoulder, hip, knee, ankle, elbow, and wrist). Synovial joints are cushioned by hyaline cartilage, lubricated by synovial fluid, and stabilized by ligaments and a joint capsule. This is anatomical background rather than a clinical condition, so this page is kept brief.

## Nutritional Relevance

General joint health is best supported by maintaining a healthy body weight (reducing mechanical load on weight-bearing joints), staying physically active, and consuming adequate protein and vitamin C to support the collagen in cartilage, ligaments, and tendons. There is no single nutrient or supplement proven to prevent joint degeneration; claims to that effect should be viewed cautiously.

## Safety Note

This is educational information, not medical advice. Joint pain, swelling, or reduced range of motion should be evaluated by a physician rather than managed through diet alone.
