## Overview

This topic covers several distinct bone conditions that share a common thread: bone mineral density and structure. Osteoporosis is a loss of bone mass and microarchitecture that increases fragility fracture risk, most common in postmenopausal women and older adults, and is treated medically with bisphosphonates, denosumab, or other bone-building drugs, plus calcium and vitamin D. Rickets (in children) and osteomalacia (in adults) result from inadequate vitamin D, calcium, or phosphate, leading to soft, poorly mineralized bone. Osteogenesis imperfecta is a genetic collagen disorder causing brittle bones, and Paget's disease involves disorganized bone remodeling. Osteoarthritis, a joint cartilage condition rather than a bone-density disorder, is covered in more depth on its dedicated page.

## Nutritional Considerations

- **Osteoporosis**: Adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, per NIH Office of Dietary Supplements guidance, is foundational alongside prescribed medication; diet alone typically cannot reverse established osteoporosis, but it supports treatment and helps prevent further loss.
- **Rickets and osteomalacia**: These are, at their core, nutritional deficiency diseases. Sufficient vitamin D (from sunlight exposure, fortified foods, or supplementation as advised by a physician) and adequate calcium and phosphate intake are the direct treatment, alongside medical management of the underlying cause.
- **Osteogenesis imperfecta**: This is a genetic collagen disorder; nutrition (adequate calcium, vitamin D, and protein) plays a supportive role, but medical treatment (often bisphosphonates) addresses the underlying bone fragility.
- **Fracture healing**: Adequate protein, calcium, vitamin D, and vitamin C support the biological healing process after any fracture from these conditions.

## Safety & When to Seek Care

This is educational information, not medical advice. Osteoporosis, rickets/osteomalacia, osteogenesis imperfecta, and Paget's disease all require diagnosis and management by a physician (often with bone density testing or blood work for vitamin D/calcium/phosphate/PTH levels). Do not attempt to self-treat suspected bone disease with supplements alone.
