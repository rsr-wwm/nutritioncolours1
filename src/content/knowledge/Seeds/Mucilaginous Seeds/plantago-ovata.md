## Overview

*Scientific name: Plantago ovata*

Psyllium husk (isabgol) is the fibrous seed-coat of Plantago ovata, used clinically as a bulk-forming fiber supplement — not eaten as a food in typical 100g quantities.

> **Serving note:** Typical clinical/dietary dose is 5–10 g (roughly 1-2 teaspoons) mixed with water, once to twice daily — composition below is a per-100g reference scale, not a dosing guide.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g reference scale)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 350 kcal |
| Fiber | 78 g |
| Protein | 1 g |

**Key nutrients:** Soluble Fiber, Insoluble Fiber


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** A well-studied bulk-forming fiber (roughly 70% soluble, 30% insoluble) used clinically for both constipation and diarrhea, and for modest LDL-cholesterol reduction.
- **[Moderate evidence]** Should be taken with a full glass of water and separated from medication dosing times, since it can slow or reduce absorption of some oral medications.

## Preparation intelligence

Psyllium must be taken with adequate water — its fiber absorbs many times its weight in liquid and can worsen constipation or pose a choking/blockage risk if taken too dry or without enough fluid.
