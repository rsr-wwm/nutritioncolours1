## Overview

*Scientific name: Salvia hispanica*

Chia seeds are a complete-fiber, omega-3-rich seed that forms a gel when soaked in liquid, owing to their mucilaginous outer coating.


## Nutrition composition (per 100 g reference scale)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 490 kcal |
| Protein | 17 g |
| Fat | 32.9 g |
| Carbohydrate | 38.3 g |
| Fiber | 33.2 g |
| Omega-3 ALA | 18.8 g |
| Phosphorus | 860 mg |
| Magnesium | 335 mg |

**Key nutrients:** Fiber, Omega-3 ALA, Phosphorus, Magnesium


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** One of the most fiber-dense common foods by weight, with roughly two-thirds of that fiber being soluble.
- **[Moderate evidence]** A strong plant source of ALA omega-3, though ALA converts to the more bioactive EPA/DHA forms only at a low, individually variable rate.

## Preparation intelligence

Unlike flaxseed, chia does not need grinding — its seed coat is soft enough to digest whole, and soaking in liquid (e.g. for chia pudding) further softens it and forms a gel from its soluble fiber.
