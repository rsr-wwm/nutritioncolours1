## Overview

*Scientific name: Sesamum indicum*

Sesame seeds (til) are notably rich in copper and calcium (particularly the unhulled form), and are a traditional winter staple across India (til laddoo, gajak).


## Nutrition composition (per 100 g reference scale)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 573 kcal |
| Protein | 17.73 g |
| Fat | 50 g |
| Carbohydrate | 11.65 g |
| Fiber | 11.8 g |
| Calcium | 975 mg |
| Copper | 4 mg |
| Magnesium | 351 mg |

**Key nutrients:** Calcium, Copper, Healthy Fat, Plant Protein

**Regional context (India):** Traditionally eaten in winter (til laddoo, gajak) — culturally tied to Makar Sankranti

## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** Among the most calcium-dense common plant foods (whole seed form), relevant for calcium intake in plant-forward diets.
- **[Moderate evidence]** An exceptionally rich dietary copper source, a mineral involved in iron metabolism and connective tissue formation.

## Preparation intelligence

Calcium figures are highest for whole (unhulled) sesame seeds; hulled/decorticated sesame (common in tahini) has substantially less calcium since much resides in the seed coat.
