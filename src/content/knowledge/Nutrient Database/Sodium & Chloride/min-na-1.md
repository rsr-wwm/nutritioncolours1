## Overview

Sodium is the main extracellular electrolyte, essential for fluid balance, nerve transmission, and muscle function. The issue in modern diets is almost always too much, not too little.

## Recommended intake

| Group | Amount per day |
| :--- | ---: |
| Adults 14+ | 1,500 mg (AI) |
| Reduce intake if above | 2,300 mg (CDRR) |

**Upper limit / risk-reduction:** Instead of a classic UL, NIH uses a Chronic Disease Risk Reduction (CDRR) target: **reduce intake if above 2,300 mg/day** to lower cardiovascular and blood-pressure risk. Most people consume well over 3,400 mg.

## Food sources

The large majority comes from processed and restaurant foods, breads, cured meats, sauces, and snacks — not the salt shaker. Naturally occurring sodium in whole foods is low.

## Deficiency

Dietary deficiency is very rare; low blood sodium (hyponatraemia) usually results from illness or excessive water intake, not diet.

## Too much

Chronically high sodium raises blood pressure and the risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney damage — the single most important electrolyte to moderate for most people.
