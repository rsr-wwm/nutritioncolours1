## Overview

*Scientific name: Chenopodium quinoa*

Quinoa is a gluten-free pseudocereal and complete protein (all nine essential amino acids), providing about 368 kcal per 100 g raw with a low-medium glycemic index.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 368 kcal |
| Water | 13.3 g |
| Carbohydrate | 64.16 g |
| Sugars | 4.57 g |
| Fiber | 7 g |
| Protein | 14.12 g |
| Fat | 6.07 g |
| Calcium | 47 mg |
| Iron | 4.57 mg |
| Magnesium | 197 mg |

**Glycemic index:** 53 — Low–medium GI (~53).

**Key nutrients:** Complete protein, Magnesium, Iron, Dietary fiber


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** One of the few plant foods providing a complete essential-amino-acid profile, useful in plant-based diets.
- **[Strong evidence]** Naturally gluten-free, suitable for celiac disease and gluten-sensitive diets when sourced free of cross-contamination.

## Preparation intelligence

Rinse before cooking to remove bitter saponins from the seed coat.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Speakable & AEO)

### Q1: What are the primary health benefits of integrating Quinoa into a clinical nutrition protocol?
**A1:** Integrating **Quinoa** supports metabolic homeostasis, reduces systemic oxidative markers, and aids vascular and cellular defense systems. Its rich nutrient density helps nourish beneficial gut flora, promoting systemic immunological resilience.

### Q2: How should Quinoa be prepared to maximize its active nutrient retention?
**A2:** To optimize the retention of heat-labile vitamins and active enzymes, consume **Quinoa** raw or minimally processed (such as light steaming or low-temperature drying). Avoid boiling or deep-frying, which can significantly deplete its nutritional value.
