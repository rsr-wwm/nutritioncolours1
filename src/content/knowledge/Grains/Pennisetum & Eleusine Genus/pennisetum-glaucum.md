## Overview

*Scientific name: Pennisetum glaucum*

Pearl millet (bajra) is a drought-hardy staple millet of western and northern India, notably rich in iron and a lower-GI alternative to refined wheat and rice.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 361 kcal |
| Water | 12.4 g |
| Carbohydrate | 67.5 g |
| Fiber | 11.3 g |
| Protein | 11.6 g |
| Fat | 5 g |
| Calcium | 42 mg |
| Iron | 8 mg |

**Glycemic index:** 55 — Low–medium GI (~55) for whole-grain bajra.

**Key nutrients:** Iron, Dietary fiber, Magnesium, Phosphorus

**Regional context (India):** Winter staple (rabi/kharif regional)

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** A notably iron-dense cereal, relevant as a dietary iron source in iron-deficiency-prone populations.
- **[Moderate evidence]** Naturally gluten-free, whole-grain millet with a lower glycemic index than refined wheat or white rice.

## Preparation intelligence

Best consumed fresh-milled; bajra flour turns rancid faster than wheat flour due to higher fat content.
