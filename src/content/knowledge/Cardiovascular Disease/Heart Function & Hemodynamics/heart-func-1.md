## Overview

Cardiac output — the volume of blood the heart pumps per minute — is determined by heart rate and stroke volume, which in turn depend on preload (how much the heart fills before contracting), afterload (the resistance the heart pumps against), and contractility (the strength of contraction). The Frank-Starling law describes how increased filling generally increases the force of contraction, up to a point. These are physiological concepts used to understand and treat heart failure and shock, not a condition treated directly with diet.

## A Note on Scope

Nutrition connects to these concepts indirectly: sodium and fluid intake affect preload (relevant in heart failure), and blood pressure — influenced by sodium intake and the DASH diet — is a major component of afterload. See our heart failure and hypertension pages for the evidence-based dietary guidance that applies to these downstream conditions.

Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
