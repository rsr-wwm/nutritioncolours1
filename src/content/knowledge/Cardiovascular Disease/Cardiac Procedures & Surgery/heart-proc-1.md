## Overview

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), commonly known as angioplasty, is a catheter-based procedure to open narrowed or blocked coronary arteries, usually with balloon dilation followed by placement of a stent to keep the artery open. It is performed by interventional cardiologists and is a standard treatment for coronary artery disease and heart attacks. Complications and follow-up care, including antiplatelet medication duration, are determined by the treating cardiology team.

## Nutrition for Recovery and Secondary Prevention

Diet does not affect the mechanics of a stent, but the same atherosclerosis that caused the original blockage can progress elsewhere without risk-factor control. After PCI, the American Heart Association recommends continuing a Mediterranean-style or DASH eating pattern, limiting saturated fat and sodium, and increasing fiber-rich foods as part of comprehensive secondary prevention, alongside prescribed antiplatelet and statin therapy.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
