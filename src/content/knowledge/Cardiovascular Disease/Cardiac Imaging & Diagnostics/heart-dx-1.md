## Overview

Echocardiography uses ultrasound to create moving images of the heart's chambers, valves, and pumping function. Transthoracic echo (TTE) is the standard non-invasive version performed through the chest wall; transesophageal echo (TEE) provides closer detail from inside the esophagus; stress echo evaluates heart function under exertion or medication-induced stress; and 3D echo adds volumetric detail, often used for valve and structural assessment. These are diagnostic tools ordered and interpreted by cardiologists.

## A Note on Scope

Echocardiography is an imaging and diagnostic procedure, not a condition managed through diet. There is no nutrition protocol that changes what an echo shows. Nutrition's relevance lies further upstream — supporting the underlying cardiovascular health (blood pressure, cholesterol, weight) that these scans are often used to monitor. See our pages on hypertension, coronary artery disease, and heart failure for evidence-based dietary guidance relevant to those conditions.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
