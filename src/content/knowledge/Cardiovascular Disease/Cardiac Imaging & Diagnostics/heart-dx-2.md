## Overview

Cardiac MRI provides detailed images of heart structure and tissue characteristics without radiation. CT coronary angiography uses contrast dye and CT scanning to visualize the coronary arteries and detect blockages or calcium buildup non-invasively. Nuclear cardiology studies (such as myocardial perfusion imaging) use small amounts of radioactive tracer to assess blood flow to the heart muscle, often during a stress test. All three are ordered by cardiologists to diagnose or characterize heart disease.

## A Note on Scope

These are diagnostic imaging modalities, not conditions with a dietary treatment. Some patients are asked to fast or avoid caffeine before certain scans — always follow your imaging center's specific pre-procedure instructions rather than general guidance. For the underlying cardiovascular conditions these scans help diagnose, see our coronary artery disease and cholesterol/hyperlipidemia pages for evidence-based nutrition guidance.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
