## Overview

Cardiac catheterization is an invasive procedure in which a thin tube is guided through a blood vessel to the heart to measure pressures, take images, or perform interventions. Coronary angiography, typically performed during catheterization, uses contrast dye and X-ray imaging to visualize blockages in the coronary arteries. Fractional flow reserve (FFR) is a pressure-based measurement taken during catheterization to determine whether a blockage is significant enough to warrant treatment. These are diagnostic and interventional procedures performed by interventional cardiologists.

## A Note on Scope

There is no diet that substitutes for or changes the results of catheterization or angiography. Nutrition's real relevance is in the underlying coronary artery disease these procedures diagnose and treat — see our coronary artery disease page for evidence-based dietary guidance on managing atherosclerosis risk before and after these procedures.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
