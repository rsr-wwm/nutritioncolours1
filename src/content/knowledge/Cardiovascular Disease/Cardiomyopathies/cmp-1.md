## Overview

Cardiomyopathy refers to diseases of the heart muscle itself. Dilated cardiomyopathy involves an enlarged, weakened left ventricle that pumps less effectively; hypertrophic cardiomyopathy involves abnormal thickening of the heart muscle, which can obstruct blood flow; restrictive cardiomyopathy involves stiff heart muscle that limits filling between beats. All three can progress toward heart failure symptoms and require specialist cardiology management, often including medication, monitoring, and in some cases devices or surgery.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Nutrition support differs somewhat by type but overlaps substantially with heart failure management once symptoms of fluid retention appear.

**Sodium and fluid awareness**: For dilated and restrictive cardiomyopathy, where fluid retention and congestion are common, clinicians frequently recommend sodium moderation and sometimes fluid tracking, similar to heart failure guidance from the American Heart Association.

**Hydration and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy**: In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, maintaining steady hydration is often emphasized because dehydration can worsen the outflow obstruction some patients experience; specific fluid goals should come from the treating cardiologist.

**General heart-healthy pattern**: Across all types, a heart-healthy eating pattern — vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean protein, and limited saturated fat and added sugar — supports overall cardiovascular health and healthy body weight, reducing additional strain on the heart.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.

When analyzing the cellular microenvironment, it is evident that targeted nutrient availability plays a crucial role in mitigating oxidative stress and inflammatory cascades.
