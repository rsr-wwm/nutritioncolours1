## Overview

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is a genetic condition causing abnormal thickening of the heart muscle, which can obstruct blood flow and increase the risk of arrhythmias, including a small but serious risk of sudden cardiac death in some patients. Management is highly individualized and led by a cardiologist, often including medication, activity guidance, implantable devices, and in some cases surgical or catheter-based procedures to relieve obstruction.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Diet does not treat the structural abnormality in HCM, but a few lifestyle factors are commonly discussed alongside medical care.

**Hydration and volume status**: Because dehydration can worsen the dynamic outflow obstruction in some forms of HCM, cardiologists often advise consistent fluid intake; specific targets should be set individually.

**Caffeine and stimulants**: Given the arrhythmia risk associated with HCM, many patients are advised to discuss caffeine, energy drinks, and stimulant intake with their cardiologist, as these can trigger palpitations in sensitive individuals.

**Weight and general cardiovascular health**: Maintaining a healthy body weight through a balanced, heart-healthy diet reduces overall cardiac workload and supports long-term cardiovascular health alongside HCM-specific medical management.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
