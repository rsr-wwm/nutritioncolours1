## Overview

An aortic aneurysm is an abnormal, weakened bulge in the wall of the aorta, the body's largest artery; if it grows large enough, it can rupture. Aortic dissection is a tear in the aorta's inner wall, allowing blood to flow between the layers — both a life-threatening emergency requiring immediate surgical evaluation. Smaller, stable aneurysms are monitored with imaging and blood pressure control, while larger or symptomatic aneurysms and any dissection require urgent or emergency surgical repair.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Diet does not treat an aneurysm or dissection directly, but blood pressure control is one of the most important modifiable factors in preventing growth and rupture.

**Sodium reduction and the DASH pattern**: Because high blood pressure places ongoing mechanical stress on the aortic wall, NIH/NHLBI's DASH eating plan and reduced sodium intake are commonly recommended alongside blood pressure medication for patients being monitored for aneurysm.

**General cardiovascular risk reduction**: A heart-healthy dietary pattern, alongside smoking cessation (a major risk factor for aortic aneurysm) and prescribed medication, supports the overall vascular health relevant to slowing aneurysm progression.

Sudden, severe chest or back pain described as tearing or ripping is a medical emergency — call emergency services immediately rather than attempting any home management.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
