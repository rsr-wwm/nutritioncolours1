# Cardiovascular Disease Nutrition

## Clinical Definition
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) encompasses a range of disorders affecting the heart and blood vessels, such as coronary artery disease, stroke, heart failure, and hypertension. Atherosclerotic plaque buildup, endothelial dysfunction, and elevated blood pressure are common pathological mechanisms.

## Nutrition
### What to Eat
- **Whole grains** – oats, barley, brown rice for soluble fiber that lowers LDL cholesterol.
- **Leafy greens & cruciferous vegetables** – kale, spinach, broccoli provide potassium, nitrates, and antioxidants.
- **Healthy fats** – extra‑virgin olive oil, avocado, nuts, and fatty fish (EPA/DHA) to improve lipid profile.
- **Legumes & beans** – high‑protein, low‑fat plant foods that reduce saturated fat intake.
- **Berries & citrus fruits** – rich in polyphenols and vitamin C for vascular health.
- **Low‑sodium options** – fresh herbs, garlic, lemon juice instead of salt.

### What to Avoid
- **Saturated & trans fats** – fatty cuts of meat, butter, fried foods, processed baked goods.
- **Excessive refined carbs** – white bread, sugary cereals, pastries that raise triglycerides.
- **High‑sodium processed foods** – canned soups, snack chips, deli meats.
- **Sugary beverages** – soda, sweetened juices, energy drinks that spike blood glucose.
- **Red and processed meat** – limit intake to reduce saturated fat and nitrates.

### Timing & Meal Planning
- **Heart‑healthy breakfast** – oatmeal topped with berries and walnuts.
- **Balanced lunch** – mixed‑green salad with grilled salmon, quinoa, olive‑oil dressing.
- **Dinner** – steamed vegetables, lentil stew, and a small portion of whole‑grain pasta.
- **Snacks** – fresh fruit, unsalted nuts, hummus with carrot sticks.
- **Portion control** – Use the plate method: half vegetables, quarter lean protein, quarter whole grains.

### Special Considerations
- **Hypertension**: Emphasize potassium‑rich foods and keep sodium < 2 g/day.
- **Hyperlipidemia**: Increase soluble fiber, omega‑3 fatty acids, and plant sterols.
- **Heart failure**: Monitor fluid intake and limit sodium; incorporate low‑sodium broth.
- **Diabetes co‑morbid**: Choose low‑glycemic index carbs and monitor carbohydrate portions.

## Frequently Asked Questions
<details><summary>What is the best oil for heart health?</summary>
Extra‑virgin olive oil, due to its high monounsaturated fat and polyphenol content.
</details>

<details><summary>How many servings of fish should I eat weekly?</summary>
Two to three servings (≈ 140 g each) of fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, or sardines.
</details>

<details><summary>Can nuts raise cholesterol?</summary>
No, nuts contain healthy fats that can improve LDL/HDL ratios when eaten in moderation.
</details>

<details><summary>Is a low‑salt diet enough to lower blood pressure?</summary>
It helps, especially when combined with potassium‑rich foods and weight management.
</details>

<details><summary>What role does fiber play?</summary>
Soluble fiber binds cholesterol in the gut, reducing absorption and lowering LDL levels.
</details>

<details><summary>Should I avoid all dairy?</summary>
Low‑fat dairy can be part of a heart‑healthy diet; avoid full‑fat versions.
</details>

<details><summary>How does alcohol affect CVD risk?</summary>
Moderate consumption (≤ 1 drink/day for women, ≤ 2 for men) may be protective, but excess increases risk.
</details>

<details><summary>Is red wine beneficial?</summary>
Resveratrol may have benefits, but evidence is modest; limit to moderate intake.
</details>

<details><summary>Can I use plant sterol spreads?</summary>
Yes, they can lower LDL when used as part of a balanced diet.
</details>

<details><summary>What about coffee?</summary>
Moderate coffee (≤ 3 cups) is generally safe and may reduce CVD risk.
</details>

<details><summary>How many fruits per day are ideal?</summary>
Aim for 2–3 servings of whole fruit daily.
</details>

<details><summary>Do I need supplements?</summary>
Only if deficiencies are identified; omega‑3 supplements can help when fish intake is low.
</details>

<details><summary>Can vegetarian diets protect the heart?</summary>
Well‑planned vegetarian diets are associated with lower CVD risk.
</details>

<details><summary>How often should I check cholesterol?</summary>
Every 4‑6 months if you have risk factors, otherwise every 1‑2 years.
</details>

<details><summary>Is night‑time eating harmful?</summary>
Late heavy meals can affect lipid metabolism; aim for earlier dinners.
</details>
