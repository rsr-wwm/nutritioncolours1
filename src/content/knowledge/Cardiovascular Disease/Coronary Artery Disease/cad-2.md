## Overview

Heart attacks are classified by their ECG and blood test findings into ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), which involves complete blockage of a coronary artery and requires immediate emergency intervention, and non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), which involves partial blockage but still requires urgent hospital treatment. Both are medical emergencies managed by cardiology teams with time-sensitive procedures and medication; the distinction guides the urgency and type of intervention, not a difference in long-term dietary management.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Once stabilized, both STEMI and NSTEMI patients generally follow the same evidence-based secondary-prevention nutrition approach.

**Mediterranean-style eating**: Cardiac rehabilitation programs, recommended after either type of heart attack, typically incorporate Mediterranean-style dietary guidance in line with American Heart Association recommendations.

**Saturated fat and cholesterol management**: Reducing saturated fat to support LDL cholesterol control remains central regardless of heart attack type.

**Consistent, moderate sodium intake**: Blood pressure management through sodium moderation supports overall cardiac recovery and reduces future event risk.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
