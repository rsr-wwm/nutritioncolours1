## Overview

A hypertensive emergency is a severe, acute blood pressure elevation accompanied by evidence of organ damage — such as to the heart, brain, kidneys, or eyes — and is a medical emergency requiring immediate treatment, typically in a hospital setting. Hypertensive urgency describes very high blood pressure without acute organ damage, which still requires prompt medical evaluation. Neither condition is managed with diet in the acute phase; both require emergency or urgent clinical care and medication.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Once a patient is stabilized, sustained dietary change becomes central to preventing recurrence.

**Long-term prevention through diet**: After an acute event, clinicians commonly reinforce the same evidence-based pattern used for chronic hypertension: the DASH eating plan, consistent sodium reduction, and limiting alcohol, as recommended by NIH/NHLBI and the American Heart Association.

**Medication adherence comes first**: Because uncontrolled hypertension and missed medication doses are common contributors to hypertensive crises, consistent medication adherence — not diet alone — is the primary prevention strategy; nutrition plays a supportive, long-term role.

If you or someone near you has a blood pressure reading with severe symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, severe headache, or vision changes, seek emergency medical care immediately rather than attempting to manage it with diet or home remedies.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
