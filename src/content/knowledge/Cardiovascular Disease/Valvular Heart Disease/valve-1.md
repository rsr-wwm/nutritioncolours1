## Overview

Aortic stenosis is a narrowing of the aortic valve that restricts blood flow from the heart, most commonly from age-related calcium buildup. Severity is graded by echocardiography, and treatment for severe, symptomatic disease includes surgical valve replacement or transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), a less invasive catheter-based procedure. Timing and choice of intervention are determined by a heart valve team.

## Nutrition-Relevant Support

Diet does not reverse valve calcification, but a heart-healthy eating pattern supports overall cardiovascular fitness going into any procedure, and weight management reduces cardiac workload on a valve that is already working harder than normal. If symptoms of fluid retention develop as the stenosis progresses, sodium moderation may be recommended, similar to heart failure guidance — discuss specific targets with your cardiologist.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
