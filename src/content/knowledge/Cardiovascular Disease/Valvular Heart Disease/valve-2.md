## Overview

Mitral regurgitation occurs when the mitral valve does not close properly, allowing blood to leak backward into the left atrium; mitral valve prolapse, a common and often benign structural variant, is one cause. Depending on severity and symptoms, treatment ranges from monitoring to surgical valve repair (generally preferred when feasible) or replacement. These decisions are made by a cardiology and cardiac surgery team.

## Nutrition-Relevant Support

There is no dietary treatment for the structural valve problem itself. If regurgitation is significant enough to cause fluid retention or heart failure symptoms, sodium moderation is commonly recommended, similar to heart failure guidance. A generally heart-healthy diet supports fitness and recovery around any planned repair or replacement procedure.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
