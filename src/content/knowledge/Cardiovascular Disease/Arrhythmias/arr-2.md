## Overview

Ventricular tachycardia (VT) and ventricular fibrillation (VF) are serious arrhythmias originating in the heart's lower chambers; VF in particular is a cause of sudden cardiac death and requires immediate emergency defibrillation. Underlying causes include prior heart attack, cardiomyopathy, and inherited electrical disorders. Treatment includes medication, implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) for high-risk patients, and catheter ablation, all directed by cardiology and electrophysiology specialists.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Electrolyte balance is one of the most clinically significant, well-established nutrition connections to ventricular arrhythmia risk.

**Potassium and magnesium**: Low blood potassium or magnesium are recognized precipitants of dangerous ventricular arrhythmias, which is why clinicians closely monitor and correct these levels, particularly in patients on diuretics or with heart failure.

**Do not self-supplement**: Because both low and high potassium can be dangerous to heart rhythm, electrolyte correction should always be directed and monitored by a physician rather than through unsupervised supplementation.

**General cardiovascular risk reduction**: Since coronary artery disease and cardiomyopathy are common underlying causes of ventricular arrhythmia, the same heart-healthy dietary patterns that reduce those risks — Mediterranean-style eating, saturated fat reduction — provide indirect, long-term support.

Sudden collapse or loss of consciousness is a medical emergency — call emergency services and begin CPR/use an AED if trained, rather than any nutrition intervention.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
