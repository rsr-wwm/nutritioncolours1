## Overview

Vascular disorders affect the blood vessels rather than the heart itself and include a range of conditions: hypertension (elevated pressure within vessels), aortic aneurysm (a weakened, bulging section of the aorta), peripheral artery disease (narrowed leg arteries from atherosclerosis), varicose veins (enlarged, twisted superficial veins, usually a venous rather than arterial issue), and vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessel walls, often autoimmune in origin). Diagnosis and treatment vary widely by condition and are managed by vascular medicine, cardiology, or rheumatology specialists as appropriate.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Several — though not all — of these conditions share a meaningful nutrition connection.

**Hypertension and PAD**: Both are strongly linked to diet: the DASH eating pattern and sodium reduction support blood pressure control, while Mediterranean-style, lower-saturated-fat eating supports the atherosclerosis underlying PAD, per NIH and AHA guidance.

**Aortic aneurysm**: Blood pressure control through diet is a recognized modifiable factor in slowing aneurysm growth, alongside blood pressure medication and smoking cessation.

**Varicose veins and vasculitis**: These are primarily structural (venous valve dysfunction) or inflammatory/autoimmune conditions without a well-established dietary treatment; general heart-healthy and anti-inflammatory eating patterns support overall health but are not a substitute for medical management.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
