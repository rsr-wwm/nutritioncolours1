## Overview

Peripheral artery disease (PAD) occurs when atherosclerosis — plaque buildup in artery walls — narrows the arteries supplying the legs and feet, reducing blood flow. Common symptoms include claudication (leg pain with walking that resolves with rest); in advanced disease, critical limb ischemia can cause pain at rest and non-healing wounds, requiring urgent vascular specialist care. PAD shares the same underlying atherosclerotic process as coronary artery disease, and people with PAD are at increased risk for heart attack and stroke.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Because PAD is driven by the same atherosclerotic process as coronary artery disease, the dietary evidence base overlaps substantially.

**Mediterranean-style eating**: A Mediterranean-style dietary pattern — rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and olive oil, with fish in place of some red meat — is supported by the American Heart Association as part of managing atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, which includes PAD.

**Saturated fat and LDL cholesterol**: Limiting saturated fat helps manage LDL ("bad") cholesterol, a key driver of arterial plaque, consistent with AHA dietary guidance.

**Fiber-rich foods**: Soluble fiber from oats, beans, and fruits supports healthy cholesterol levels as part of an overall heart-healthy pattern.

**Working alongside exercise therapy**: Supervised exercise therapy is a cornerstone PAD treatment; nutrition support for weight management and vascular health complements — but does not replace — this and other prescribed treatments, including smoking cessation support where relevant.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
