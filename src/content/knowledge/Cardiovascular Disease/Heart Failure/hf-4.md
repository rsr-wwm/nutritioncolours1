## Overview

Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) occurs when chronic heart failure suddenly worsens, causing significant fluid buildup and severe symptoms such as shortness of breath, often requiring hospitalization for intravenous diuretics and close monitoring. Common triggers include dietary sodium excess, medication non-adherence, infection, or new cardiac events. This is a medical emergency requiring hospital-level care, not a condition managed by diet alone in the acute phase.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Because excess sodium and fluid intake are among the most common preventable triggers of ADHF, dietary discipline plays a genuinely important role in preventing recurrence.

**Strict sodium limitation**: Following the sodium restriction guidance set by your cardiology team is one of the most effective ways to prevent recurrent fluid overload and rehospitalization, per American Heart Association recommendations.

**Individualized fluid limits**: Patients with a history of ADHF are often given a specific daily fluid allowance by their care team, which should be followed precisely rather than estimated.

**Daily weight and symptom tracking**: Consistent daily weighing helps catch early fluid retention before it progresses to another acute decompensation episode, allowing earlier medication adjustment.

If you experience sudden worsening shortness of breath, rapid weight gain, or significant swelling, contact your care team or seek emergency care promptly rather than waiting.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
