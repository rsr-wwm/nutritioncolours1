## Overview

Heart failure means the heart cannot pump or fill effectively enough to meet the body's needs, leading to symptoms like shortness of breath, fatigue, and fluid retention. It is classified by ejection fraction — HFrEF (reduced), HFmrEF (mildly reduced), and HFpEF (preserved) — which guides medication choice, since some therapies are proven most effective in specific categories. Diagnosis and treatment are managed by cardiology, typically combining medication, monitoring, and lifestyle measures.

## Nutritional & Dietary Considerations

Across all heart failure types, nutrition management centers on controlling the fluid and sodium balance the failing heart struggles to regulate.

**Sodium restriction**: The American Heart Association recommends limiting sodium intake in heart failure to reduce fluid retention and the workload on the heart; specific targets should be individualized with your cardiology team, as needs vary by severity.

**Fluid awareness**: Some patients, particularly with more advanced heart failure, are given individualized fluid intake guidance by their care team to prevent overload; this is not a universal restriction and should be set by a physician.

**Daily weight monitoring**: Tracking daily weight is a standard, low-burden way to catch early fluid retention before it becomes symptomatic, allowing earlier intervention.

**Heart-healthy foundation**: Beyond sodium and fluid, a generally heart-healthy pattern — vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean protein — supports overall cardiovascular health and helps manage any underlying coronary disease contributing to heart failure.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This information is educational and is intended to support, not replace, care from your cardiologist or physician. Blood pressure and cholesterol targets, medication decisions, and treatment plans must be individualized by your care team. Dietary changes work alongside — not instead of — prescribed cardiovascular medication and monitoring.
