## Overview

*Scientific name: Brassica oleracea var. sabellica*

Kale is a cruciferous leafy green providing roughly 49 kcal per 100 g raw, carrying among the highest vitamin K and vitamin C densities of any common vegetable.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 49 kcal |
| Water | 84 g |
| Carbohydrate | 8.75 g |
| Sugars | 2.26 g |
| Fiber | 3.6 g |
| Protein | 4.28 g |
| Fat | 0.93 g |
| Calcium | 150 mg |
| Iron | 1.47 mg |
| Vitamin C | 120 mg |
| Vitamin K | 705 µg |

**Glycemic note:** GI not typically assessed for kale — low digestible carbohydrate per serving.

**Key nutrients:** Vitamin K, Vitamin C, Vitamin A (carotenoids), Calcium


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** A very high dietary vitamin C and vitamin K source relative to its low calorie density.
- **[Limited evidence]** As a cruciferous vegetable, kale contains glucosinolates; individuals on certain thyroid medications or with iodine-deficiency risk are sometimes advised to moderate very large raw intakes — a clinical, individualized question, not a general restriction. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Massaging or lightly cooking raw kale breaks down its cell walls, softening its fibrous texture for easier digestion.
