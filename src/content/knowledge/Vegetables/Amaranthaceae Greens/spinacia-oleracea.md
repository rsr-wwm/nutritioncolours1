## Overview

*Scientific name: Spinacia oleracea*

Spinach is a leafy green providing about 23 kcal per 100 g, an exceptionally dense source of vitamin K, iron, and folate, with negligible impact on blood sugar.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 23 kcal |
| Water | 91.4 g |
| Carbohydrate | 3.63 g |
| Sugars | 0.42 g |
| Fiber | 2.2 g |
| Protein | 2.86 g |
| Fat | 0.39 g |
| Calcium | 99 mg |
| Iron | 2.71 mg |
| Vitamin C | 28.1 mg |
| Vitamin K | 483 µg |

**Glycemic note:** GI not clinically meaningful — negligible digestible carbohydrate per typical serving.

**Key nutrients:** Vitamin K, Iron, Folate, Provitamin A (carotenoids)

**Regional context (India):** Winter (Nov–Feb) in most regions; year-round in cooler hill areas

## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** One of the most concentrated common dietary sources of vitamin K, relevant to normal blood clotting function.
- **[Moderate evidence]** A source of non-heme iron, though its oxalate content limits bioavailability relative to animal-source iron; pairing with vitamin C may improve absorption.

## Preparation intelligence

Spinach is high in oxalates, which can bind non-heme iron and calcium and reduce their absorption; pairing with vitamin-C-rich foods or brief cooking can help.
