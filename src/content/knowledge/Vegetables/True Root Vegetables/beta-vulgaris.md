## Overview

*Scientific name: Beta vulgaris*

Beetroot provides about 43 kcal per 100 g raw and is notably rich in dietary nitrates and folate; cooking raises its glycemic index from a low ~32 raw to a moderate ~64.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 43 kcal |
| Water | 87.6 g |
| Carbohydrate | 9.56 g |
| Sugars | 6.76 g |
| Fiber | 2.8 g |
| Protein | 1.61 g |
| Fat | 0.17 g |
| Folate | 109 µg |
| Potassium | 325 mg |

**Glycemic index:** 64 — Raw beetroot GI ≈32; boiled ≈64 (moderate) as cooking breaks down fiber structure — glycemic load of a standard serving stays modest (~7) given typical portion size.

**Key nutrients:** Dietary nitrates, Folate, Potassium, Betalains

**Regional context (India):** Winter (Nov–Feb)

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** A useful dietary folate source, relevant to normal cell division and particularly notable in preconception and prenatal nutrition contexts.
- **[Limited evidence]** Dietary nitrates from beetroot have been studied for modest blood-pressure and exercise-performance effects; results vary by dose, form (juice vs. whole vegetable), and individual. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Beetroot's dietary nitrates are partly converted to nitric oxide in the body, a pathway studied for supporting blood pressure and exercise performance — effects are dose- and study-dependent.
