## Overview

*Scientific name: Solanum tuberosum*

Potato (boiled in skin) provides about 87 kcal per 100 g and is a genuinely high-glycemic-index food — unlike the carrot myth, potato's high GI is well-supported — meaning portion and preparation matter for blood-sugar-aware eating.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 87 kcal |
| Water | 77 g |
| Carbohydrate | 20.13 g |
| Fiber | 1.8 g |
| Protein | 1.87 g |
| Fat | 0.1 g |
| Potassium | 379 mg |
| Vitamin C | 13 mg |
| Vitamin B6 | 0.27 mg |

**Glycemic index:** 82 — Boiled potato GI is genuinely high (~78–96 depending on variety and prep, per International GI tables); cooling boiled/baked potato for 24 hours before eating forms resistant starch and measurably lowers its glycemic impact.

**Key nutrients:** Potassium, Vitamin C, Vitamin B6, Resistant starch (when cooled)

**Regional context (India):** Winter harvest (Jan–Mar), stored year-round

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** A meaningful dietary potassium and vitamin C source, notable because potato is eaten in large quantities in many diets.
- **[Moderate evidence]** Potato's glycemic index is genuinely high and preparation-sensitive; cooling cooked potato before eating measurably increases resistant starch and lowers post-meal glucose response versus eating it hot.

## Preparation intelligence

Boiling or steaming with skin on, then cooling before eating (or reheating), meaningfully raises resistant starch and lowers the practical glycemic impact versus fresh-hot mashed or baked potato.
