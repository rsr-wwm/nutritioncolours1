## Overview

*Scientific name: Solanum lycopersicum*

Tomato provides about 18 kcal per 100 g raw and is the leading common dietary source of the carotenoid lycopene, which becomes more bioavailable when tomatoes are cooked with a fat source.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 18 kcal |
| Water | 94.5 g |
| Carbohydrate | 3.89 g |
| Sugars | 2.63 g |
| Fiber | 1.2 g |
| Protein | 0.88 g |
| Fat | 0.2 g |
| Vitamin C | 13.7 mg |
| Potassium | 237 mg |

**Glycemic note:** GI not typically assessed as clinically meaningful — very low digestible carbohydrate per serving.

**Key nutrients:** Lycopene (carotenoid), Vitamin C, Potassium

**Regional context (India):** Year-round, peak Dec–Mar

## What the evidence says

- **[Moderate evidence]** The leading common dietary source of lycopene, a carotenoid studied for antioxidant activity.
- **[Limited evidence]** Population-level research associates tomato and lycopene intake with cardiovascular and prostate health markers; causal, dose-specific claims remain an active research area. *(study citation under clinical review)*

## Preparation intelligence

Cooking tomatoes (e.g. into a sauce) with a small amount of oil breaks down cell walls and increases lycopene bioavailability compared with raw tomato.
