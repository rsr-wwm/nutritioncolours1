## Overview

*Scientific name: Glycine max*

Soybeans — cooked, mature — provide about 172 kcal per 100 g and are the only common plant food providing a complete essential amino acid profile in a single, unprocessed source.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g, cooked unless noted)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 172 kcal |
| Carbohydrate | 8.4 g |
| Protein | 18.21 g |


**Key nutrients:** Complete Plant Protein, Isoflavones, Fiber, Iron


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** One of the very few plant foods providing a complete essential-amino-acid profile comparable to animal protein sources.
- **[Moderate evidence]** Contains isoflavones (plant phytoestrogens) studied for menopausal symptom and cardiovascular research; effects are modest and dose-dependent, not equivalent to hormone therapy.

## Preparation intelligence

Fresh green soybeans (edamame, steamed in the pod) and mature dried/cooked soybeans are the same species at different harvest maturity; edamame is milder and higher in moisture, mature soybeans are used for tofu, tempeh, and soy milk.

## Detailed Clinical & Research Intelligence

### 1. Physiological Pathways & Bioactive Mechanisms
The active therapeutic agents in **Soybeans** modulate key physiological processes to support cellular health and metabolic function. Specifically, secondary plant metabolites, key mineral cofactors, and targeted trace vitamins play direct roles in supporting enzymatic cascades and minimizing cellular oxidation. Regular integration under guided protocols helps stabilize vascular endothelial function and supports natural systemic defense networks.

### 2. Clinical Efficacy & Evidence-Based Applications
Recent clinical observations suggest that dietary elements contained within **Soybeans** provide supportive benefits for glycemic control and glycemic index management. Active fibers and organic compound matrixes slow gastric absorption, preventing abrupt glycemic spikes and stabilizing plasma levels. In chronic disease management, these properties offer secondary support to baseline therapeutic interventions.

### 3. Bioavailability Optimization & Synergy
To maximize the systemic absorption of key compounds in **Soybeans**, preparation methods should avoid prolonged exposure to high thermal values, which can denature sensitive active enzymes. Pairing **Soybeans** with high-quality plant lipids (such as organic cold-pressed oils) significantly enhances the bioavailability of fat-soluble vitamins and active phytochemicals.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Speakable & AEO)

### Q1: What are the primary health benefits of integrating Soybeans into a clinical nutrition protocol?
**A1:** Integrating **Soybeans** supports metabolic homeostasis, reduces systemic oxidative markers, and aids vascular and cellular defense systems. Its rich nutrient density helps nourish beneficial gut flora, promoting systemic immunological resilience.

### Q2: How should Soybeans be prepared to maximize its active nutrient retention?
**A2:** To optimize the retention of heat-labile vitamins and active enzymes, consume **Soybeans** raw or minimally processed (such as light steaming or low-temperature drying). Avoid boiling or deep-frying, which can significantly deplete its nutritional value.

### Q3: Are there any specific contraindications or lifestyle factors to consider?
**A3:** While **Soybeans** is highly beneficial as a dietary supplement, therapeutic amounts should be coordinated with a clinical nutritionist. Individuals with specific metabolic sensitivities should monitor biomarker baselines during regular integration.
