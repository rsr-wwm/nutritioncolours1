## Overview

*Scientific name: Phaseolus vulgaris*

Kidney beans (rajma) — cooked — provide about 127 kcal per 100 g and are a fiber-dense staple of North Indian cuisine (rajma-chawal); raw/undercooked kidney beans contain a natural toxin destroyed by proper boiling.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g, cooked unless noted)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 127 kcal |
| Carbohydrate | 22.8 g |
| Fiber | 6.4 g |
| Fat | 0.5 g |
| Protein | 8.7 g |
| Folate | 130 µg |

**Glycemic index:** 24 — Low GI (~24), one of the lowest of common pulses.

**Key nutrients:** Fiber, Folate, Plant Protein, Iron


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** A fiber- and folate-dense plant protein source at a low glycemic index.
- **[Strong evidence]** Must be fully boiled (not merely simmered) to inactivate phytohaemagglutinin — a genuine, well-documented food-safety requirement, not a general caution.

## Preparation intelligence

Raw or undercooked kidney beans contain phytohaemagglutinin, a natural toxin causing gastrointestinal illness; a full rolling boil for at least 10 minutes (not just a low simmer or slow-cooker warmth) is required to destroy it — dried beans should always be boiled hard before finishing in a slow cooker.
