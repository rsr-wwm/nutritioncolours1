## Overview

*Scientific name: Vicia faba*

Fava beans (broad beans) — cooked — provide about 110 kcal per 100 g and are a Mediterranean/Middle Eastern staple; they carry a well-documented, clinically important risk of triggering favism in people with G6PD deficiency.

## Nutrition composition (per 100 g, cooked unless noted)

| Nutrient | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Energy | 110 kcal |
| Water | 72.1 g |
| Carbohydrate | 19.8 g |
| Fiber | 5.4 g |
| Fat | 0.4 g |
| Protein | 7.7 g |


**Key nutrients:** Plant Protein, Fiber, Folate, Iron


## What the evidence says

- **[Strong evidence]** A reasonable plant protein and fiber source for the general population, but a genuine, serious contraindication exists for individuals with G6PD deficiency — screening or a known-safe history matters before recommending fava beans clinically.

## Preparation intelligence

People with G6PD deficiency (an inherited enzyme condition, more common in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, African, and South/Southeast Asian populations) can develop acute hemolytic anemia ("favism") from eating fava beans — this is a genuine, well-documented clinical contraindication, not a general food sensitivity.
