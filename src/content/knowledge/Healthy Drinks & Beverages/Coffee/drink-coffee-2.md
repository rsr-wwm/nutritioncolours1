## Overview

Espresso is coffee brewed by forcing hot water through finely ground, tightly packed coffee under pressure (roughly 9 bar), producing a small, concentrated shot topped with crema. Milk-based drinks build on this base: a cappuccino is traditionally equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk foam; a latte uses more steamed milk and a thin layer of foam; a flat white uses a higher coffee-to-milk ratio with fine "microfoam" and little to no foam cap. An Americano is espresso diluted with hot water, closer in strength to drip coffee.

## Nutrition and practical considerations

The main nutritional variable across these drinks isn't the coffee itself but the **milk and add-ins**. A straight espresso or Americano has negligible calories; a large latte made with whole milk can add roughly 150–220 calories depending on size, and flavored syrups can add substantial sugar — a "12 oz flavored latte" can easily exceed the added sugar most guidelines recommend for a day. Choosing skim or lower-fat milk, smaller sizes, or unsweetened plant milks (oat milk foams well and is closest in texture to dairy; almond and coconut milk foam less easily and add fewer calories) are practical ways to moderate calorie and sugar intake while still enjoying espresso drinks.

Caffeine content per shot of espresso is generally lower than a full cup of drip coffee (roughly 63 mg per single shot vs. ~95 mg per 8 oz drip cup), though larger or multi-shot drinks can exceed drip coffee's caffeine total.

## Safety and contraindications

- **Lactose intolerance:** Traditional milk-based espresso drinks can trigger symptoms in lactose-intolerant individuals; lactose-free or plant milks are common substitutes.
- **Added sugar:** Flavored syrups and whipped toppings can turn a coffee order into a significant source of added sugar; the American Heart Association recommends limiting added sugar to about 25 g/day for women and 36 g/day for men.
- **Caffeine:** Multi-shot drinks (e.g., a "doppio" latte) can add up quickly toward daily caffeine limits.
