## Overview

Peppermint tea is made from the leaves of *Mentha × piperita*, a hybrid of watermint and spearmint. It is caffeine-free and traditionally used after meals to ease digestion. Its cooling sensation comes from **menthol**, which activates the TRPM8 cold-sensing receptor.

## Active compounds and evidence

Most of the clinical evidence on peppermint and digestion comes from studies of **enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules** rather than the brewed tea, so effects are generally stronger and better documented for the oil. NCCIH notes that peppermint oil has shown benefit for symptoms of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) — reducing abdominal pain and bloating — in a number of clinical trials. The tea itself is widely used for mild indigestion, gas, and nausea, largely on the strength of traditional use and menthol's known antispasmodic action on gut smooth muscle.

## Safety and contraindications

- **GERD/acid reflux:** Peppermint relaxes the lower esophageal sphincter, which can worsen heartburn and reflux in susceptible people.
- **Hiatal hernia:** Similar caution applies.
- **Infants and young children:** Menthol-containing preparations (particularly concentrated oil) should not be applied to or given to infants, as menthol can trigger a choking/laryngospasm reaction; food-amount tea for older children is generally considered safe.
- **Gallstones:** Peppermint stimulates bile flow and may trigger discomfort in people with gallstones.

## How it's typically prepared

1–2 teaspoons of dried leaves (or a small handful of fresh leaves) steeped in hot water for 5–10 minutes.
