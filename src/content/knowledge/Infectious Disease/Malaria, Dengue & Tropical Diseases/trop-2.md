## Overview

This topic covers several mosquito-borne and viral hemorrhagic diseases important in tropical medicine and travel health. Dengue, caused by any of four related viruses (serotypes 1–4) spread by Aedes mosquitoes, can progress from mild fever to severe dengue with plasma leakage and bleeding. Zika virus, also spread by Aedes mosquitoes and through sex, is generally mild but can cause serious birth defects, including microcephaly, when a pregnant woman is infected. Chikungunya causes fever and often severe, prolonged joint pain. Ebola virus disease is a rare but severe and often fatal illness spread through contact with infected body fluids, causing fever, vomiting, diarrhea, and in some cases hemorrhage.

## Nutritional considerations

For dengue, chikungunya, and Zika, there is no specific antiviral treatment, so supportive care — rest, hydration, and fever or pain management as directed by a clinician — is central to recovery; oral fluids are especially important in dengue given the risk of plasma leakage and fluid shifts in severe cases. Ebola causes profound fluid and electrolyte losses through vomiting and diarrhea, and oral or intravenous rehydration is a critical part of clinical management alongside other supportive care. During recovery from any of these illnesses, adequate protein and calorie intake supports tissue repair and the return of strength, and small, frequent meals can help while appetite is still recovering. Pregnant women with possible Zika exposure should follow their obstetric provider's guidance closely, as nutrition alone cannot prevent congenital Zika syndrome.

## Safety note

Nutrition and hydration support the body through these illnesses but do not substitute for medical care. Severe dengue, Ebola, and high fever with bleeding or neurological symptoms in any of these diseases require immediate medical attention. Do not take aspirin or NSAIDs for suspected dengue without medical advice, as they can increase bleeding risk.
