## Overview

Encephalitis is inflammation of the brain, most often caused by viral infection (such as herpes simplex virus) or, less commonly, by the immune system mistakenly attacking the brain (autoimmune encephalitis, including anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis). It is a medical emergency — symptoms include fever, headache, confusion, seizures, or altered consciousness, and require immediate hospital evaluation with brain imaging, spinal fluid testing, and antiviral or immune-modulating treatment as appropriate.

## Nutrition during recovery

Recovery from encephalitis can be prolonged and, depending on severity, may involve rehabilitation for cognitive or physical deficits. During hospitalization, patients who cannot swallow safely may need tube feeding, managed by the clinical team. During longer-term recovery, adequate protein and overall calorie intake support tissue healing and rehabilitation, and any specific swallowing difficulties (dysphagia) should be assessed by a speech-language pathologist before diet texture is adjusted.

## Safety

Encephalitis requires emergency medical treatment; nutrition support is adjunctive and should be directed by the treating medical and rehabilitation team, not self-managed. Anyone with sudden confusion, fever, seizures, or severe headache needs immediate emergency care.
