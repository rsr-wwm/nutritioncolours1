## Overview

Kimchi is a Korean fermented vegetable dish, most commonly made from napa cabbage and Korean radish, seasoned with gochugaru (Korean chili flakes), garlic, ginger, scallions, and often a fermented fish or seafood component (jeotgal) for umami. UNESCO recognized the practice of making and sharing kimchi (kimjang) as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2013. There are well over 100 regional and seasonal varieties.

## Fermentation process

Cabbage is brined for several hours, rinsed, and coated in a seasoning paste before being packed tightly and fermented — typically a few days at room temperature followed by slower, longer fermentation under refrigeration. The microbial community shifts over time, starting with *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* and shifting toward *Lactobacillus* species (including *L. plantarum* and *L. brevis*) as fermentation progresses and the kimchi becomes more sour.

## Nutritional profile and evidence

Kimchi supplies vitamin C, vitamin K, and fiber from the cabbage and radish base, alongside live lactic acid bacteria when unpasteurized. It has one of the more actively studied probiotic profiles among fermented vegetables: research has examined its lactic acid bacteria for potential roles in gut microbiome diversity, and cruciferous-vegetable compounds (isothiocyanates) it shares with raw cabbage for broader metabolic effects. As with most fermented-food research, much of the human evidence is preliminary or based on small trials, so specific disease-prevention claims should be treated as an active research area rather than settled fact.

## Safety and dietary notes

- Traditional kimchi typically contains fish sauce or fermented shrimp and is **not vegan or vegetarian by default** — vegan versions substitute kelp, shiitake, or soy-based umami sources; always check labels.
- Kimchi is relatively high in sodium from the brining and seasoning process, relevant for anyone managing blood pressure.
- Store-bought pasteurized kimchi will not contain live cultures; unpasteurized, refrigerated kimchi does.
